# Pass 150 — Law No.708 Article 13, sanctions, and successor-law audit

Date: 2026-09-25

## Result

This pass moved from the official enactment-reason summary to the operative statutory text. The National Law Information Center historical text verifies that Article 13 of the Education Temporary Special Act (Law No.708) prohibited teachers in national, public, and private schools from labor movements and from collectively refusing classes. Article 22(1)(3) attached a criminal sanction of up to six months imprisonment or a fine up to one million hwan to Article 13 violations.

The official amendment history shows no deletion or amendment of Article 13 during the 1962-1963 amendment sequence. Article 19 was revised in April 1962; Articles 7-8 were deleted in September 1962; Article 10 and Article 15 were changed and Article 16 deleted in February 1963; Article 21 was deleted in April 1963. Law No.708 itself was repealed by Law No.1466 on 1963-12-05 with repeal effective 1964-01-01. Therefore the Article 13 prohibition remained textually present through the end of the statute's life, while actual enforcement frequency remains OPEN.

The pass also verified the broader institutional architecture around Article 13. Article 9 centralized appointment procedures for national/public university leadership and faculty; Article 10 imposed faculty qualification screening; Articles 11-12 regulated private-school staff appointment and dismissal; Articles 17-20 regulated school-foundation governance. National Archives' Private School Act topic history states that the latter private-school provisions formed a basic framework for the 1963 Private School Act. School Reorganization Standards Decree No.283 directly implemented Law No.708 Article 3(2).

## Critical uncertainty preserved

No evidence found in this pass proves that the 1961-06-21 `교직단체법` became Law No.708, was merged into it, or supplied Article 13. The policy overlap is real; textual/procedural lineage remains OPEN until the June bill original and cabinet disposition are obtained. Likewise, the existence of Article 13 plus a criminal penalty does not prove that any named teacher-union activist was prosecuted under Article 13. Indictments and judgments remain required.

The repeal of Law No.708 also must not be described as automatic restoration of the 1960 legal position. The 1963 general public-service framework developed separate restrictions on public officials' labor and collective activity. Successor treatment must therefore be decomposed into national/public teachers, private-school teachers, Education Public Officials Act, National Public Service Act, and Private School Act tracks.
