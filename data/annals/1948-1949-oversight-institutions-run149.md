# Run149 fourth-pass: ordinary state institutions and legal authority

The 1948 commission organization decree (no.2, 1948-08-30) placed the commission under the President and required majority commission decisions for discipline. The chair had a casting vote in a tie. A list of nine signatures in the 1949 February case is therefore not a substitute for the original attendance and vote record.

The Audit Office bill (1948-11-11 original Assembly transcript) dealt with examination of state accounts and parliamentary revisions to the government's draft. It is a different institution from the commission investigating official misconduct. The 1949 intelligence bureau (Presidential Decree no.61, 1949-03-03) was under the Prime Minister and collected information about rebellion and foreign aggression. It must not be conflated with the commission.

The Civil Service Discipline Decree no.202, Gazette no.197 dated 1949-10-15, created special and ordinary disciplinary committees, required documented charges and notification of commission or criminal proceedings, and specified implementation of committee decisions. Its October date prevents retroactive use as authority for February decisions.

Source anomaly: the March 1949 intelligence bureau decree lists a guidance division in article 7, but describes an investigation division in article 10. Check Gazette original for transcription or drafting error.

These four sources widen the project beyond disputed scandals to normal administrative structures, fiscal accounting, staffing, legal procedure and inter-agency division of labor. Original Gazette facsimiles remain pending.
