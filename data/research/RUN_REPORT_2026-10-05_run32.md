# Run 32 — 2026-10-05

## Scope
Continued the Rhee Syngman backbone from Run 31 by drilling into Korean-side contemporaneous records for the 1952 Pusan political crisis. The focus was actor separation: Won Yong-dok's own account of the Assembly bus detention, Yi Jong-chan's Army-wide neutrality order, the legislature's immediate inability to convene, and the July 4 amendment procedure. Floot was not modified or built.

## Material findings
1. **Won Yong-dok's own public account creates a new attribution boundary rather than closing the command question.** His May 27 statement says the May 26 bus was stopped at the temporary Central Government building, later towed to the 70th Military Police unit, and that after identities were established the list was reported 'upward' and action followed 'instructions'. Crucially, he did not identify that superior or instruction issuer. This is now stored as party testimony, not neutral fact.
2. **The bus incident had an immediately observable legislative consequence.** A contemporary Dong-A Ilbo report preserved by NIKH says the Assembly attempted an emergency meeting on its normal recess day because of roughly 50 absent/affected members, but only 56 had appeared by 13:00. This supports disruption of legislative functioning without by itself proving the detention's subjective purpose.
3. **Yi Jong-chan is now an independent command/political actor in the crisis graph.** Army HQ Order No.217 of May 27 explicitly framed the Army as not belonging to any institution or individual and warned troops against political involvement. The document directly supports his institutional position, but it does not by itself prove any particular refusal of a presidential troop-deployment order.
4. **The Korean contemporary July 4 account gives procedural detail absent from compressed summaries.** It reports an 20:00 opening, whole-committee agreement report, no substantive debate, compressed readings, and a standing vote of 166 present / 163 for. The National Assembly stenographic original remains a priority.
5. **The hybrid nature of the first amendment is independently visible in Korean contemporary description.** The report lists direct presidential election, bicameralism, cabinet no-confidence, and prime-minister recommendation for appointment/dismissal of ministers, and even flags internal constitutional tensions. This reinforces Run 31's correction against modeling the amendment as a one-clause direct-election event.
6. **A contemporary Korean/foreign-wire item records the government's communist-conspiracy justification for nine legislator arrests.** It is stored explicitly as a government claim, not as proof that the alleged conspiracy existed.

## Actor / graph implications
- **원용덕 (Won Yong-dok):** strengthened as an independent operational/command node. His own statement admits an upward-report / instruction chain while leaving its endpoint unnamed. That ambiguity is historically valuable and is preserved.
- **이종찬 (Yi Jong-chan):** should be promoted from a background military figure to a crisis actor node when the politician/actor registry next migrates, because an authored Army-wide order directly documents his institutional stance.
- **국회 집단행위:** the May 27 attempted emergency sitting is a distinct sub-event from the May 26 bus detention.
- **신익희 / 김종순:** the July 4 contemporary account identifies Shin Ik-hui as presiding at final declaration and Kim Jong-sun as explaining the clauses; these are concrete legislative-role leads, not yet full politician promotions in this run.
