# Claim note — Private School Act Article 58 labor-movement deletion

- claim_id: `clm-private-school-art58-labor-deletion-2005`
- status: `VERIFIED_TEXT_AND_STATED_RATIONALE / CAUSAL_GENEALOGY_OPEN`
- law: Private School Act, Law No.7802
- promulgated: 2005-12-29
- effective: 2006-07-01
- affected provision: Article 58(1)(4)

## Verified proposition

Law No.7802 removed `노동운동` from the Article 58(1)(4) list of private-school teacher dismissal grounds. The National Law Information Center's official amendment reason itself identifies exclusion of labor movement from dismissal grounds as one component of the reform.

Official source: https://www.law.go.kr/LSW/lsSideInfoP.do?ancNo=07802&ancYd=20051229&chrClsCd=010202&lsNm=사립학교법&urlMode=lsRvsDocInfoR

Accessed: 2026-09-25.

## Temporal layers

1. 1963 Law No.1362: Article 58(1)(4) expressly included `정치운동 또는 노동운동`.
2. 1981-version provision: still contained the labor-movement ground; this is the provision identified in Constitutional Court 97헌바61.
3. 2005 Law No.7802: promulgated deletion of `노동운동`.
4. 2006-07-01: deletion took effect.
5. Later versions: Article 58(1)(4) continues without `노동운동`.

## Evidence roles

- supporting evidence: MOLEG official amendment text and reason for Law No.7802.
- contemporaneous legal observation: Constitutional Court 97헌바61 confirms the older labor-movement clause remained legally salient in the pre-deletion regime.
- later statutory cross-check: 2008/2011 and current versions omit the phrase.
- insufficient evidence: no committee record yet proves why this specific deletion entered the 2005 reform at the actor/causal level.
- contradicting evidence: none found for the date/law-number deletion claim.

## Guardrails

Do not state that the 1999 Teacher Union Act itself deleted the Private School Act clause. Do not state that union legalization caused the 2005 deletion until committee/drafting records establish the connection. Do not treat deletion of one dismissal ground as repeal of every legal restriction on teacher collective action, because Article 55 incorporation and other public-service/teacher-union rules form separate legal paths.
