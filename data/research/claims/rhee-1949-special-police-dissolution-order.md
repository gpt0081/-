# Claim — Rhee order for dissolution of Anti-National Activities Special Committee special police

- claim_id: `clm-rhee-1949-special-police-dissolution-order`
- status: `VERIFIED_NARROW_COMMAND_ATTRIBUTION`
- date_of_public_attribution: 1949-06-07
- event: `evt-1949-06-07-rhee-order-admission`

## Verified proposition

The National Institute of Korean History's Republic of Korea chronology records that on 1949-06-07 President Rhee Syng-man announced that the police dissolution of the Special Committee's special police unit was carried out on his order.

Supporting official record: https://db.history.go.kr/id/tcct_1949_06_07_0010 (accessed 2026-09-26).

A separate contemporary-source transcription records Seoul police chief Kim Tae-seon saying on June 6 that the government had repeatedly identified the special police as illegal and instructed its dissolution, and describes the disarmament/recovery of weapons, equipment and vehicles: https://db.history.go.kr/id/dh_012_1949_06_06_0050 (accessed 2026-09-26).

The National Assembly's contemporaneous response treated the raid/dissolution as a grave executive-legislative conflict and demanded restoration of the special police and cabinet resignation: https://db.history.go.kr/contemp/level.do?levelId=dh_012_1949_06_06_0150 (accessed 2026-09-26).

## Pass 157 stenographic narrowing

The 1949-06-06 National Assembly stenographic record adds two distinct layers. Interior Vice Minister Jang Gyeong-geun formally reported deployment of roughly forty Seoul police and the disarmament/arrest of twenty-two special-police personnel, while defending the operation on the theory that the special police lacked formal state-police appointment. In the same debate, Committee chairman Kim Sang-deok stated that the prime minister, interior minister and army chief had previously discussed the President's special-police dissolution position and agreed on conversion of the personnel into ordinary police. Source: https://db.history.go.kr/item/cons/level.do?levelId=cons_001_0020_0020_0030_0020_0080 (accessed 2026-09-26).

This materially narrows the next transmission-chain target: the missing bridge is no longer merely an abstract `Rhee → police` link. It is the documentary record, date and participants of the high-level consultation described by Kim Sang-deok, followed by the Interior/Seoul-police operational order that converted policy into the June 6 deployment. Kim's statement is contemporaneous parliamentary testimony, but until cabinet, Interior Ministry, Army HQ or Blue House records corroborate it, it remains evidence of the asserted consultation rather than proof of its exact form.

## Scope guardrail

This evidence is sufficient to attribute the decision to dissolve/disarm the Special Committee special police to Rhee at the level of his own public acknowledgment. It is not sufficient, without further operational records, to attribute every arrest, search, seizure, physical act, or regional implementation detail of the June 6 police action directly to him.

The June 6 stenographic record reinforces this separation. Jang described the official operation as dissolution/disarmament and said mistakenly received non-special-police documents were returned. Kim Sang-deok and Kim Myeong-dong alleged broader searches, document removal, movement restriction and other excesses. Those competing contemporaneous accounts require item-level police/prosecutorial records and cannot be silently collapsed into the presidential order.

## Earlier policy continuity

NIKH chronology records Rhee statements on 1949-01-10 urging minimized enforcement of the Anti-National Activities Punishment Act and on 1949-02-02 urging limits on Special Committee arrests. The Constitutional History DB also indexes the 1949-02-16 presidential speech `특경대는 폐지하라, 특위 체포 못한다`. These establish a documented policy conflict before June, but motive and operational causation must still be separately proven.

## Contradiction / adversarial status

No source opened in Pass 157 contradicted the narrow proposition that Rhee publicly acknowledged ordering dissolution of the special police. The stenographic record instead reveals a sharper legal and factual conflict over (a) whether the separately named special police possessed lawful status, (b) whether prior Interior Ministry agreements regularized it, and (c) whether the June 6 operation exceeded the dissolution/disarmament order. These remain separate claims.
