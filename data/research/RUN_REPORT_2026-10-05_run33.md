# Run 33 — 2026-10-05

## Scope
Continued the Rhee Syngman backbone from Run 32, but widened the 1952 Pusan-crisis frame in both directions: the constitutional-policy chain before martial law and the constitutional/administrative consequences after the July 4 vote. The principal gain is a reusable 24-document Korean official/contemporaneous ledger rather than another compressed narrative. Floot was not modified or built.

## Material findings
1. **The constitutional confrontation has a documented pre-crisis executive chain.** The January 18 government direct-election amendment failed 19–143 with one abstention. A separate contemporary account records sustained opposition debate, including Eom Sang-seop's institutional objection to bicameralism and executive-legislative conflict. This is stronger than treating the January vote as a bare number.
2. **Rhee's April 8 cabinet instruction is an attributable policy act.** National Archives' display of Cabinet Meeting No.27 records his instruction that direct presidential election and bicameralism be implemented within the deadline. This belongs upstream of the May coercive phase and should not be collapsed into it.
3. **The cabinet then institutionalized opposition to the Assembly's amendment.** Cabinet Meeting No.28 records a report on a rebuttal to the Assembly amendment and agreement to publish anti-amendment arguments in newspapers. This is an executive-policy sub-event distinct from police/martial-law action.
4. **The Assembly's rival amendment can be tied to a formal Gazette object.** Government Notice No.35, published May 7, preserves the Assembly proposal and its dissolution/cabinet-responsibility provisions. This makes the constitutional conflict a document-to-document contest, not simply 'direct election versus cabinet system'.
5. **The May 28 UN intervention identifies a concrete executive group at the presidential meeting.** The contemporary record says the defense, interior and justice ministers and martial-law commander attended Rhee's meeting with UNCOK. This creates a cabinet-level actor cluster without proving that every attendee shared responsibility for each coercive act.
6. **The Assembly's May 28 martial-law termination demand is now actor-resolved.** Kim Jong-sun and 17 others moved it; Kwak Sang-hun argued for it; the vote was 96–3 with 139 present. This makes Kim Jong-sun more than a July 4 clause explainer and supports an independent legislative node.
7. **Chang Taek-sang's May 28 report gives an administrative account of the anti-Assembly demonstrations.** He traced demonstrations on May 19, 21, 23 and 24 and stated that the Busan police chief had permitted the May 19 assembly after a pledge of peaceful conduct. This is evidence about administrative knowledge/permission, not proof of who organized or financed the demonstrations.
8. **The May 29 Assembly record materially expands the bus-detention evidence.** Shin Ik-hui, Kim Dong-sung and So Son-gyu reported on the incident; the Assembly sought attendance of the prime minister, defense minister, martial-law commander and public-information chief; and the contemporary item contains a 45-member passenger list. The passenger list is a high-value politician-discovery source for the next migration pass.
9. **The government and external observers described the same crisis differently.** Rhee's June 2 reply to UNCOK preserved the communist-conspiracy framing as an investigation claim, while the JOINT WEEKA report characterized the period as increasingly extra-legal pressure and police control. Neither is silently promoted to neutral omniscient fact.
10. **Normal governance continued during the crisis and must remain visible.** On June 2 the Public Information Office announced Chang Taek-sang's 46-person policy committee spanning industry, finance, education/culture, diplomacy, medicine, journalism and academia. This is useful evidence against building a corpus made only of constitutional violence.
11. **Legislative leadership itself became provisional.** On June 18 the Assembly postponed regular speaker elections and selected Shin Ik-hui, Cho Bong-am and Kim Dong-sung as temporary presiding officers until political conditions normalized. Cho Bong-am therefore connects agrarian reform and the 1952 constitutional crisis through separate institutional roles.
12. **Seo Min-ho's July 1 death sentence is a distinct judicial/martial-law event.** The court applied Martial Law Act Article 16 and Criminal Code Article 199. Foreign diplomatic and UN observers were reported present. This should be modeled separately from the arrests of legislators for alleged communist conspiracy.
13. **The July 3 security environment is directly described in Korean contemporary material.** The report distinguishes members who stayed overnight, members escorted by police, and members who appeared voluntarily, while recording police checks at the temporary Central Government entrance and police deployment around the chamber. It does not establish the subjective state of every member.
14. **The July 4 procedural record has competing contemporaneous emphases.** One report says the extracted clauses passed after an open whole-committee consultation; another stresses the absence of substantive debate and unusually compressed passage. These are not necessarily mutually exclusive, so the claim remains procedure-contested rather than forcing one description to erase the other.
15. **The National Assembly Archives provides a clean procedural spine.** Kwak Sang-hun and 122 others proposed the Assembly amendment on April 17, it was announced May 7–June 5; the government proposal was made and announced May 14; both were introduced June 21; the vote occurred July 4; promulgation July 7. This chronology should anchor event decomposition.
16. **The executive promulgation chain is now first-class documentary evidence.** National Archives identifies Cabinet Meeting No.55 as deciding promulgation and Gazette No.688 as the July 7 promulgation object, plus a signed first-amendment original transferred from the Ministry of Government Legislation to presidential records.
17. **Rhee's July 8 cabinet instruction exposes an important post-amendment institutional tension.** The newly amended constitution contained cabinet-responsibility/checking elements, yet the next day's cabinet record says Rhee instructed that it still be operated as a presidential-centered system and warned against divided authority or executive instability. This is direct evidence of executive interpretation, not by itself proof of later illegality.
18. **The August 5 election evidence contains an internal adversarial pair.** A Busan Ilbo account described voting as occurring in a guaranteed free atmosphere, while a Dong-A Ilbo item the same day reported investigations involving Lee Beom-seok campaign workers and a serving police middle manager for election-law violations. The corpus should preserve both instead of using either as a total description of the election.
