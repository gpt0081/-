# Rhee pass 67 — Robertson bargain convergence, 1953-07-01~11

Access date: 2026-09-22
Scope: FRUS 1952–1954, Korea XV/2, documents 653–697, with emphasis on the transition from bargaining crisis to conditional armistice cooperation. Floot untouched.

## Core findings

1. The July bargain was not a simple mutual-defense-treaty swap. Robertson's July 3 aide-memoire combined a treaty, ROK force levels, economic rehabilitation, political-conference procedure, POW disposition, and continued UNC command relations.
2. Dulles explicitly rejected Rhee's requested advance pledge to resume war after a failed political conference on constitutional grounds: such a war could not be promised by the President without Congress.
3. The proposed 20-division ROK force level was contested inside the U.S. chain. Clark noted that it reflected wartime planning, while JCS post-hostilities concepts contemplated a smaller army. Thus `20 divisions` must be represented as a negotiated/contested force-planning commitment, not a timeless settled U.S. policy.
4. U.S. coercive planning and diplomatic bargaining ran in parallel. Clark considered withdrawal signaling, troop movements, POW consolidation, supply slowdown, suspension of equipment for four additional ROK divisions, and psychological pressure on ROK commanders. Robertson warned that a withdrawal bluff was dangerous if Washington would not actually carry it out.
5. Rhee's July 7 aide-memoire sought a Japan-treaty-style security arrangement, flexibility above 20 divisions, joint withdrawal from the political conference after 90 days if it failed, renewed fighting or at least U.S. moral/material support, revised POW arrangements, and preservation of an ultimate sovereign option to alter the UNC relationship.
6. On July 8 Rhee told Robertson he had never intended an immediate ROK withdrawal from UNC and wanted the current command relationship to continue after armistice, while reserving a sovereign right to change it later. This materially narrows any claim that Rhee's armistice opposition necessarily meant immediate operational secession from UNC.
7. Senate ratification was itself a bargaining variable. Dulles and Senate leaders tied confidence in treaty ratification to ROK cooperation and avoidance of unilateral military adventure. Knowland was reported as saying there was no time for Senate action in the remaining weeks of the current session.
8. The July 6 U.S. draft treaty and Rhee's July 7 counter-position show treaty text evolving during the bargain. Rhee pushed toward the U.S.-Japan model; Washington was willing to consider related language but did not accept a blank substitution of Korea for Japan.
9. Pyun Yong-tae emerges as a substantive Korean-side drafting actor. Robertson believed Pyun drafted the July 7 aide-memoire and characterized him as obstructing agreement. This is a U.S. contemporaneous assessment, not yet an independently verified fact about authorship or intent.
10. The bargain cannot be reduced to personalities. Briggs, Young, Murphy, Clark, Dulles, Smith, Senate leaders, JCS and military commanders each shaped different constraints: drafting, political assessment, command feasibility, constitutional authority, ratification and coercive contingency planning.

## Adversarial checks

- Claim: Rhee demanded only a defense treaty. **Contradicted** by multi-part July 7 counterproposal.
- Claim: U.S. promised automatic renewed war if political conference failed. **Contradicted** by Dulles July 1 constitutional rejection and consultation language.
- Claim: 20 ROK divisions were a settled postwar U.S. force structure. **Contradicted/narrowed** by Clark's July 3 note on JCS post-hostilities planning.
- Claim: Rhee intended immediate withdrawal of ROK forces from UNC. **Contradicted for July 8 stated position**, while later sovereign reservation remains.
- Claim: treaty ratification was guaranteed. **Contradicted**; Senate action remained constitutionally independent and politically conditional.
- Claim: Washington uniformly favored bluffing Rhee with withdrawal. **Contradicted**; Robertson warned against a threat Washington would not execute.
- Claim: coercive military planning replaced negotiation. **Contradicted**; both proceeded simultaneously.
- Claim: Pyun's authorship/obstruction is established Korean fact. **Insufficient**; currently a Robertson attribution requiring Korean provenance.
- Claim: Korean air/naval expansion was precisely fixed. **Contradicted/narrowed**; U.S. wording deliberately avoided implying a balanced force of specified size.
- Claim: Rhee simply accepted the U.S. draft. **Contradicted**; July 7 counter-draft sought substantive modifications.
