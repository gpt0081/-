## Scope

## Material findings
1. NIKH `dh_013_1949_07_06_0120` fixes the amendment vote to **1949-07-06, 4th session, 3rd plenary**. The adopted Kwak Sang-hun et al. amendment changed Article 29 so limitation completed at the end of August 1949; vote: present 136, yes 74, no 9.
2. This same contemporary record materially corrects an over-simple chronology: on 6 July the chair already reported resignation intentions from eight commissioners — Cho Jung-hyeon, Lee Jong-sun, Oh Gi-yeol, Cho Gyu-gap, Kim Sang-deok, Park Woo-gyeong, Kim Jun-yeon, Kim Gyeong-bae — and three special prosecutors. Kim Sang-deok stated that most commissioners had been submitting resignations since **7 June**. Two attempts to accept the resignations were inconclusive, so they were not accepted that day.
3. Therefore the common formula `amendment passed -> next day commissioners first resigned` is too coarse. A safer graph is: June resignation submissions already existed; 6 July Assembly consideration did not accept them; after the limitation amendment the old commission then moved into a full-resignation/reconstitution phase. The exact 7 July formal act remains a separate primary-record target.
4. A Kim Koo Museum paper points specifically to the 7 July resignation announcement and cites `조선중앙일보 1949-07-08` plus `국회속기록 제5호 pp.45-49`. This is now the precise primary target rather than a generic search.
5. A contemporary 29 Aug statement by second-chair Lee In, reproduced from the 31 Aug Kyunghyang Shinmun, says that on **14 July** Lee In as chair and Song Pil-man as vice-chair were among ten commissioners newly elected/confirmed by the Assembly. This anchors the reconstitution date independently of later summaries, though the original 14 July stenographic vote remains to be obtained.
6. NIKH primary file `an_043_0040` (21 Aug) carries the signatures/seals of Song Pil-man, Cho Heon-yeong, Yu Jin-hong, Jin Jik-hyeon, Cho Jung-hyeon and Cho Guk-hyeon and is addressed to chair Lee In. This directly verifies six members of the second commission in active case disposition.
7. Pass 195's secondary roster stated four re-elected commissioners (Cho Gyu-gap, Cho Jung-hyeon, Lee Jong-sun, Kim Gyeong-bae) and six newly elected (Lee In, Yu Jin-hong, Song Pil-man, Cho Heon-yeong, Cho Guk-hyeon, Jin Jik-hyeon). The 21 Aug primary opinion independently verifies five of the six named new members plus re-elected Cho Jung-hyeon; Lee In is verified as recipient/chair. Lee Jong-sun's August primary opinions remain independent evidence of re-elected service.
8. NIKH modern-person record `im_109_02182` identifies **Jin Jik-hyeon (晉直鉉)**, born circa 1900/1901, from Imsil, Jeonbuk, as a National Assembly member and legal professional. This supplies a stable biography anchor for a second-commission member surfaced in pass 195.
9. National Assembly Library `PROC2014000269`, 20 Sep 1949, independently records Cho Guk-hyeon, Cho Heon-yeong, Yu Jin-hong and Cho Gyu-gap speaking in the 5th-session plenary, useful for extending these people beyond a single anti-collaboration event into ordinary parliamentary activity.
10. National Assembly Library `PROC2014000299`, 4 Nov 1949, records Cho Guk-hyeon and Cho Heon-yeong in debate on the Education Act / trademark / vested-property agenda, with Cho Heon-yeong acting as deputy Industry Committee chair. This is a concrete bridge back to ordinary-governance coverage rather than keeping these nodes controversy-only.

## Adversarial / correction notes
- **Correction A:** `all commissioners resigned only after the 6 July amendment` is not safe. Resignation submissions pre-dated the vote, with Kim Sang-deok reporting submissions since 7 June.
- **Correction B:** `6 July resignation acceptance` is false according to the contemporary record; the Assembly failed to accept the eight commissioner / three prosecutor resignations that day.
- **Correction C:** popular summaries disagree on the amendment vote count (e.g. 114-9 vs 74-9). The NIKH-hosted contemporary Kyunghyang report gives **present 136 / yes 74 / no 9** and is retained as the stronger anchor pending original stenographic tally.
- **Guardrail:** Lee In's 29 Aug statement is a participant statement reproduced in a contemporary newspaper. It is strong evidence for the 14 July reconstitution but does not substitute for the original plenary vote record.
- **Guardrail:** later evaluative labels such as `pro- or anti-purge` are not imported as registry facts without action-level primary evidence.
