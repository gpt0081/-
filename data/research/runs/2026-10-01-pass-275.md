## Material advance
The strongest new actor-level finding is a cabinet split that prevents treating the executive as a unitary actor. FRUS telegrams 1252 and 1259 report Prime Minister Chang Taek-sang saying that Rhee pressed the cabinet to endorse dissolution of the National Assembly, while most cabinet members refused. After Lightner delivered Truman's message, Rhee backed away from dissolution at least temporarily. This creates a distinct `opposed_by` layer inside the cabinet rather than attaching the dissolution threat generically to the Government of the Republic of Korea.

Telegram 1259 adds a separate, narrower coercion claim: Chang told Embassy officers that Home Minister Yi Pom-sok proposed arresting all Assembly members and putting them in a stockade. The repository now records this as a **reported proposal**, not an executed order. Its provenance is contemporaneous but indirect: US Embassy officers reporting Chang's account. A Korean cabinet minute, Yi paper, or direct statement remains required before raising the evidentiary status.

The US/UNC intervention line was also adversarially tightened. Documents 1171, 894, CX 69393, 899, 1228, 1229, 881, 1243, DEF 910410 and 907 show a real menu of contemplated pressure: Assembly protection, economic-aid withholding, naval or troop signaling, ROK Army/Naval action, and at the outer edge UNC martial law/military government. But Clark, State and Defense repeatedly distinguished contingency from decision, emphasized lack of troops and military risk, and treated military intervention as a last resort. A simple claim that Washington had already decided to overthrow or militarily replace Rhee is therefore not supported by this document chain.

The same chain also complicates a one-directional US-democracy narrative. Telegram 907 states that US/UN interests might best be served if Rhee remained president under controls and in a chastened mood, and even viewed popular election as potentially improving his standing. This sits alongside sharp US objections to coercion and martial law. Both lines are preserved because the policy record contains them simultaneously.
