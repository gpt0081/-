## Key findings and corrections
1. Contemporary chronology records the May 16 seizure as a military action followed by Military Revolutionary Committee proclamations and the seizure of legislative, executive, and judicial power. The model therefore does not collapse the coup into a Park Chung-hee single-person order.
2. On May 19 the Military Revolutionary Committee became the Supreme Council for National Reconstruction, with Jang Do-young as chair, Park Chung-hee as vice-chair, and a large roster of military members. The first institutional node is therefore collective even though later power concentration must be separately traced.
3. Economic-development planning did not begin from zero after the coup. Official historical material preserves a First Republic three-year plan and a Second Republic five-year-plan draft alongside the military government's plan. Continuity and revision must be tested item by item.
4. The 1962 constitutional transition had multiple procedural stages: review, public hearings, drafting, publication, Supreme Council vote, and December 17 referendum. Procedure and political competitiveness are recorded as different claims.
5. The Democratic Republican Party's formal founding record names Chung Ku-young as president and Kim Chung-yul as party chair. Kim Jong-pil's organizational influence must therefore be represented separately from formal party office.
6. The June 3 crisis is split into anti-Korea–Japan-talk protest actors, the government's martial-law decision, and Gen. Min Ki-sik's martial-law command execution.
7. The 1965 Basic Treaty is not treated as identical to the earlier Kim Jong-pil–Ohira memorandum. Negotiation memoranda, final treaty text, parliamentary ratification, opposition records, Japanese diplomatic records, and US observations are separate evidence objects.
8. Vietnam deployment is modeled as government proposal → National Assembly consent → Defense Ministry/military execution, with financial and US-support arrangements as a separate claim bundle.
9. The third-term amendment is split among ruling-party advocacy, parliamentary procedure, referendum, and opposition coalition. The July 17 opposition committee record identifies Kim Jae-jun as chair and a wider advisory network including Yun Posun, Yu Jin-o, Ham Seok-heon, Park Soon-chun and others.
10. October Yushin is not a one-day event. The next pass must split the October 17 emergency measures, martial-law execution, National Assembly dissolution, constitutional drafting/approval, referendum, and National Conference for Unification institutional design.
