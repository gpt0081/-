## Material advance
The main correction is significant. Pass272 left the Korean martial-law decree/appointment chain pending. A directly opened NIHK record reproducing Defense Minister Shin Tae-young's May 24 statement now supplies the domestic public command structure: emergency martial law effective 00:00 May 25; Army Chief of Staff Lt. Gen. Lee Jong-chan named martial-law commander; Maj. Gen. Won Yong-deok separately appointed Southern District martial-law commander for the listed Gyeongsangnam-do areas including Pusan. This corroborates Won's appointment independently of FRUS and adds Lee's formal overall command role.

A second Korean record opens Won Yong-deok's May 27 public account of the May 26 National Assembly bus detention. Won said the bus failed to comply with a checkpoint at the temporary central government building, was towed to the 70th Military Police unit, occupants initially withheld identities, a roster was made, the result was reported upward, and after instructions they were allowed to act freely. This is valuable because it exposes an internal reporting chain. It is also a party/executor account. The identity of the superior who issued the decisive instruction remains unknown and is now a sharply targeted primary-document lead.

The government's stated rationale is also clearer: Shin cited renewed guerrilla activity and rear-area security after martial law had been relaxed for local elections; Won cited guerrilla activity, alleged red-cell agitation in Pusan, and Pusan's importance as a logistics base. Those claims are preserved as government justification, not independently verified necessity. They conflict in interpretation with the US Embassy/UNC contemporaneous assessment that Pusan martial law lacked military necessity and was being used for political coercion. The repository should preserve that provenance conflict rather than silently choose one narrative.

The run also separated the Army martial-law chain from the Interior/police chain. A Korean May 24 report confirms Yi Pom-sok's appointment as State Council member and Home Minister replacing Chang Sok-yun. FRUS editorial reconstruction identifies Hong Pom-hui as Vice Home Minister and 'Yun-Kyong' as Chief of National Police immediately before proclamation. The latter two still need Korean appointment originals and identity disambiguation before canonical promotion.

Finally, the crisis graph was decomposed further. So Min-ho's May 25 arrest, the May 26 Assembly-bus detention, other legislator arrests, the May 28 Assembly termination resolution, and later US/UNC protective-surveillance planning are distinct events with different actors and authority questions. Lee's overall martial-law title is therefore not treated as proof that he personally ordered the bus detention, and Rhee's political authority is not treated as proof of personal authorship of each arrest order.
