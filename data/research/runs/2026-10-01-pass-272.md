## Material advance
The largest correction is that Directive No.217 is no longer merely a primary target. NIHK `dh_025_1952_05_27_0050` reproduces the directive, dates it 1952-05-27, identifies Army Headquarters in Daegu, addresses it to all unit commanders, and carries Army Chief of Staff Lt. Gen. Lee Jong-chan's signature. Its institutional content is unusually clear: the military is not subordinate to an institution or individual, must remain outside political affairs, must preserve the chain of command, and must concentrate on national defense.

That primary text also imposes an important limit. It does **not** name Rhee, Won Yong-deok, martial law, or a particular troop-transfer request. It therefore proves Lee's formal military-neutrality position but cannot by itself be labeled a written refusal addressed to Rhee.

FRUS document 144 materially narrows the missing command chain. On May 27 Van Fleet told UNCURK that neither he nor the ROK Army Chief of Staff had been consulted in advance about martial law. He told Rhee that he would not approve movement of additional combat troops to Pusan because of the effect on the front. Rhee's own explanation, recorded in the same telegram, was that the cabinet had decided on martial law, he had suggested including Pusan, urgency prevented written Chief-of-Staff orders, the Defense Minister would issue the decree, Won Yong-deok would command martial law, and Won would take orders directly from the President. Rhee also showed Van Fleet papers he said were Lee-to-Won/other-officer messages countermanding the prior oral arrangement. Those alleged messages are now a sharply defined primary target rather than a vague lead.

A later November FRUS conversation with Gen. Lemnitzer adds another command layer: Rhee sought two Korean divisions for the Pusan martial-law operation, Van Fleet refused, and one company was apparently authorized and moved. This means the troop issue must distinguish Korean constitutional command, ROK Army chain of command, and UNC operational control. It is unsafe to narrate the episode as a single Rhee→Lee request/refusal chain.

The domestic legislative side was also cross-checked. NIHK's May 28 record reports that the National Assembly passed a demand to lift Pusan martial law 96-3 with 139 present, after debate referring to the forced detention of more than forty legislators' vehicle. This gives a Korean contemporaneous provenance family independent of FRUS. FRUS May 30 then records US/UNCURK pressure to lift martial law and release Assembly members, alongside Rhee's own defense that the arrests were an internal matter.

Finally, June 2-3 FRUS reporting identifies internal cabinet resistance. Prime Minister Chang Taek-sang reported that most cabinet members refused to endorse Rhee's proposed dissolution of the National Assembly, and Rhee temporarily backed down. The executive branch therefore should not be modeled as politically uniform.
