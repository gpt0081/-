## Material advance
The June 3 retreat from immediate dissolution must not be mistaken for termination of the coercive phase. Muccio's June 14 telegram reports martial law still in force, continuing Assembly intimidation, talk of further arrests, active political squads, and renewed provincial demonstrations. The repository therefore now separates `temporary retreat from dissolution` from `continuing coercive environment`.

Muccio's June 16 cable adds an important uncertainty rule. He warned that further deterioration or Rhee incapacity could produce a takeover by Home Minister Yi Pom-sok. This is stored only as a foreign contingency assessment. It is not promoted into a claim that Yi had an operational coup/succession plan.

The June 18 and June 23 records further constrain simplistic descriptions of US policy. Muccio explicitly stated intervention required Washington orders, while the Embassy continued to seek a Korean compromise. His June 23 letter says the governing instruction still contemplated Rhee remaining in office under controls and describes discussions with the Prime Minister, Assembly chair/vice-chair, and DNP leadership. Thus foreign opposition to coercion coexisted with a policy preference to avoid direct regime replacement.

A new allied-policy layer was added from June 24. British representatives proposed nine points: constitutional compromise, lifting martial law, releasing arrested legislators subject to later lawful proceedings, civil/public trials, free elections, no political purge/death sentences, removal of advisers such as Yi Pom-sok, insulation of the Army from political appointments, and ending anti-foreign propaganda. State took the proposal under consideration but immediately raised the enforcement problem if Rhee refused. This is proposal-level evidence, not an adopted ultimatum.

Finally, Rhee's June 25 letter to Truman is preserved as first-person counterevidence/context. Rhee justified refusal to lift martial law and release accused Assemblymen by alleging a Communist conspiracy, denied intending dictatorship, and said he still sought a solution without dissolving the Assembly. The conspiracy allegation remains unverified here and is explicitly queued for court-record and later-finding checks.
