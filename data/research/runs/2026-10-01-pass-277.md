The July 4 vote is also decomposed. FRUS records a 166-member plenary, a special-committee compromise package, and a 163-0 vote with three abstentions. The package combined direct popular election of President/Vice-President and bicameralism with limited cabinet responsibility. The National Institute of Korean History constitutional-history material adds a distinct later-finding layer: it describes coercive pressure and procedural defects, including absence of prior public announcement, while noting that legality was not openly challenged at the time. Formal enactment and procedural validity are therefore stored as separate claims.

The post-vote branch was narrowed with July 9 and July 12 US records. State regarded the crisis as resolved 'for the time being' but still had no confirmation that Pusan martial law had been lifted. Press censorship had been lifted and VOA relay restored. Muccio then reported broad relief but said the compromise was widely recognized as a Rhee victory and that opposition figures still hoped for lifting martial law and restoration of a freer atmosphere. Thus `amendment passed` is no longer treated as synonymous with `coercive environment instantly ended`.
