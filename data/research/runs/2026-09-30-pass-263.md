4. Immediately before martial law, records identify Yi Pom-sok as Interior Minister, Hong Pom-hui as vice minister and Yun U-gyeong as police chief. This narrows the institutional command map but does **not** resolve who signed or orally issued each individual arrest order.
5. Won Yong-deok is separately represented as martial-law commander. Ministerial, police and martial-law command authority are not collapsed into one actor.
6. The National Assembly's June 18 temporary-chair arrangement adds Sin Ik-hui, Cho Bong-am and Kim Dong-seong as procedural actors during the crisis rather than treating the legislature as a faceless body.
7. The July 4 amendment vote and July 7 promulgation are separated. National Archives gives 166 present / 163 in favor for the vote and identifies direct presidential election, bicameralism and the no-confidence mechanism among the core changes.
8. The August 5 presidential election is linked to four candidate nodes: Rhee, Cho Bong-am, Yi Si-yeong and Sin Heung-u. National Archives, NEC and NIHK public-institution trails converge on the election date and principal result totals.
9. The vice-presidential contest is now a distinct event. NIHK chronology preserves a broader candidate network led by winner Ham Tae-yeong, followed by Yi Pom-sok, Cho Byeong-ok, Yi Gap-seong, Yi Yun-yeong, Jeon Jin-han, Baek Seong-uk and Jeong Gi-won.
10. The June 25 assassination attempt is split into immediate act, government investigation/allegation and court process. Yu Si-tae's attempted shooting is established by the immediate contemporary report; later Interior Ministry claims naming Kim Si-hyeon, Seo Sang-il, No Gi-yong and others remain allegation-layer evidence until matched to indictment/judgment.
11. The International Club case is kept separate from the assassination-attempt prosecution. A contemporary August 15 report states all 30 related persons received non-prosecution dispositions, so earlier arrest/investigation is not allowed to masquerade as a conviction.
12. The local-election line is now a deliberate anti-president-centric expansion route: first-generation local councilors can become independent politician nodes once identity, affiliation and institutional activity are verified.
