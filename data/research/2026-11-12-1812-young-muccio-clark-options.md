## Findings, rival explanations and action candidates

1. d178 is a **policy memorandum** weighing non-intervention, diplomatic pressure, economic sanctions and conditional UNC protective measures. It does not prove that any proposed sanction or military intervention was approved or executed. The memorandum expressly questions the legal basis for indefinitely withholding an Economic Coordination Agreement payment and describes a roughly $35 million initial payment.
2. d178 distinguishes political assessment of Rhee/Lee Bum-suk influence from verified command records. Assertions of Lee's efforts to control armed forces are US officials' contemporary assessments, not Korean appointment or operational orders.
3. d179 reports Muccio's assessment that Rhee had not moved toward conciliation, that martial law remained, and that groups identified as provincial council representatives came to Pusan. It does not establish who organized each group or whether provincial councils formally voted for Assembly dissolution.
4. d179 **proposes** a meeting among Muccio, UNCURK, Clark and Van Fleet on June 16, and a joint appeal. Proposal does not establish that the meeting occurred.
5. d180 reports Clark's contrary preference for a diplomatic rather than direct military pressure phase (in editorial note citing Clark's June 15 cable CX 50221). Muccio explicitly clarifies he sought a friendly appeal rather than active military pressure and shifts toward Van Fleet acting alone. Thus a unified US military/diplomatic intervention decision cannot be inferred.
6. d180's suggestion that Yi Pom-suk could take over after Rhee's possible incapacity is Muccio's scenario, not evidence of an attempted takeover. Rhee's health observations are likewise the ambassador's impressions, not a medical finding.
7. d180 mentions prior May 27 and June 2 interviews as reported in telegrams 1170 and 1243. These are independent *documents* but within the same US edited family and should not inflate provenance counts.
