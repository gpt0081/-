## Findings
1. Panmunjom's final obstacle was not simply whether Rhee had privately promised non-obstruction. d686–d698 show a separate military-negotiation problem: what UNC could truthfully guarantee about ROK governmental/ROKA compliance, logistics, and coercion.
2. d687 distinguishes three propositions: Rhee's qualified political assurance, UNC authority over ROKA, and whether U.S./UNC would cut logistics to a violating ROK force. They were debated separately.
3. d688 shows even the July 11 formula that UNC would not support aggressive ROKA action failed initially to satisfy the Communist delegation.
4. d693–d694 show Clark wanted to use Rhee's July 9 letter at Panmunjom, but Robertson objected to textual use because its qualifications and confidentiality could damage relations with Rhee. The joint statement could be used instead.
5. d695–d698 show UNC considered the Communist demand for definite guarantees a real negotiating obstacle. The final U.S. formula asserted UNC capacity to carry out the armistice without promising coercive force against the ROK government.
6. d699 (NSC 156/1) makes economic reconstruction a national-security policy layer, including ROK force expansion and institutional/economic rehabilitation. It should not be collapsed into armistice diplomacy.
7. d701 records Clark's critical assessment of II ROK Corps performance after the July Communist offensive and identifies Korean corps commander Chung. This is a military-command node/lead, not evidence about civilian political responsibility.
8. d703–d705 reveal a renewed Korean-government dispute after the July 19 Communist summary. Paek Tu-chin and Pyun Yung-tai challenged Harrison's statements about ROK compliance and NNRC/Indian troops. Briggs reports Pyun did most of the talking while Paek supported the substantive demands.
9. d704 is an embassy interpretation of Rhee/Pyun intentions, not Korean-origin proof. Its claim that they retained a plan to resume war after an unsuccessful political conference must remain contemporaneous-observer evidence.
10. d707 documents a public-communications rupture: Robertson complained that Rhee's separate press statement undercut the agreed joint communiqué and specifically questioned why Pyun had bound the U.S. side to a delayed release while Rhee's separate statement was already out. This creates a distinct publicity/translation/release-chain lead.
11. d709 provides a useful role split: Paek interpreted the Dulles–Robertson message's implications in the meeting; Briggs then reported Pyun separately briefing correspondents and threatening that ROK would not remain bound absent satisfaction. Briggs's phrase that Pyun was trying to 'torpedo' the armistice is an observer judgment, not neutral fact.
12. d692 broadens the corpus beyond war termination. Rhee complained about aid-agency overhead, duplication, scarce buildings, coordination, and reconstruction output. This creates reconstruction/public-administration/aid-governance research lines.
13. d699 similarly strengthens economy, force-building, fiscal/aid, and institutional reconstruction coverage.
14. d706 shows the Joint Policy Declaration/Greater Sanctions issue linked alliance deterrence, airfield rehabilitation, allied diplomacy, and assurances Robertson had given Rhee. It is a multinational-policy object, not a bilateral treaty substitute in a legal sense.
15. d713–d715 show Rhee again asked on July 24 for automatic defense and U.S. participation or support if the political conference failed. Eisenhower/Dulles explicitly preserved constitutional processes and U.S. freedom of action. Thus July 9/11 non-obstruction did not erase the underlying dispute.
16. d717 and d719 show Rhee subsequently reaffirmed his pledge while arguing that Harrison's Panmunjom language might have undermined the conditions/understandings on which he believed it rested.
17. d716/d718/d720/d723 separate signing logistics from political consent: senior delegates signed at Panmunjom, commanders countersigned, ceasefire took effect 12 hours later, and the ROK was not a signatory.
18. d722 records Clark, Taylor and Briggs informing Rhee before signature, with Paek and Pyun present; Rhee congratulated Clark. This is strong contemporaneous evidence against a simplistic claim that Rhee was unaware of the final signing timetable.
19. d724–d725 document immediate transition from armistice to reconstruction/security-pact/political-conference diplomacy and Dulles's planned Korea visit.
20. The July 10–27 sequence therefore needs at least four linked state machines: Panmunjom assurances; Korean internal/public positioning; military command/ROKA compliance; postwar reconstruction/security diplomacy.

3. `July 11 UNC assurance immediately settled Communist objections` → contradicted by d688.
4. `ROK government and ROKA command were legally identical questions` → contradicted by repeated separate treatment d686–d698.
5. `Paek and Pyun were passive attendees after Robertson left` → contradicted by d703/d709.
6. `Pyun's anti-armistice intent is proven by Korean documents` → not proven; d704/d709 are Briggs interpretations/reports.
7. `Rhee had abandoned automatic-defense and renewed-war demands by July 11` → contradicted by d713.
8. `U.S. promised automatic war participation after 90 days` → contradicted by d714/d715.
9. `ROK signed the July 27 armistice` → contradicted by d720/d723 signing sequence.

12. `Joint Policy Declaration was legally the same as the bilateral mutual-defense treaty` → unsupported; d706 treats it as separate multinational deterrent declaration.
