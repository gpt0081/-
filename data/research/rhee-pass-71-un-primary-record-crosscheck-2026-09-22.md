## Findings

### 1. A/2450 independently fixes the force-contributor proposal's institutional design
UN-indexed A/2450 text states that A/L.151 was submitted 16 August by Australia, Belgium, Canada, Colombia, Ethiopia, France, Greece, Netherlands, New Zealand, Philippines, Thailand, Turkey, UK and US, with Luxembourg joining on 17 August as A/L.151/Rev.1. It proposed participation by willing Unified Command troop contributors together with the Republic of Korea. Crucially, the participating governments were to act independently and be bound only by decisions/agreements to which they adhered. This is stronger evidence against modeling a single monolithic `UN side` actor.

A/2450 also says the United States, after consultation with the participating countries, should arrange with the other side a conference no later than 28 October 1953 at a mutually satisfactory time/place. Thus the event graph needs separate edges for GA recommendation, U.S. arranging function, participant consultation, and inter-side agreement.

### 2. The UN record sharpens the India-vote chronology
The UN 1953 Yearbook records that the First Committee adopted the India-participation draft A/L.153 as a whole by roll-call 27–21–11. It also records separate paragraph votes and a rejected Soviet amendment A/C.1/L.53. The resumed-session Index then states that draft resolution C in A/2450, concerning India, was not voted on in plenary at India's request on 28 August. This independently corroborates FRUS d752's withdrawal account and makes `India was voted down/excluded by the UN` false.

### 3. U.S. opposition to India was explicitly framed as a belligerent/non-belligerent rule
The Yearbook says the U.S. representative argued that the conference was likeliest to succeed if limited to belligerents on both sides and that the U.S. did not oppose India as India, but opposed inclusion of any non-belligerent. This does not prove that Rhee's opposition was irrelevant; it does show that the public UN rationale cannot be reduced to `Washington simply obeyed Rhee`.

### 4. Opposition to India was broader than the ROK/U.S. pair
The Yearbook lists representatives including Bolivia, Brazil, China, Colombia, Cuba, Dominican Republic, El Salvador, Greece, Haiti, Israel, Philippines, Peru and the United States among those opposing Indian participation. This is an important adversarial correction to any bilateral causal story. The precise motives of each delegation remain separate open questions.

### 5. Soviet participation was conditional, and the committee rejected Soviet deletion attempts
The Yearbook records the USSR amendment A/C.1/L.53 against A/L.152/Rev.2, including an attempt to delete `provided the other side desires it`; that deletion was rejected 36–15–8. A/L.152/Rev.2 was then adopted as a whole 55–2–2 in committee. UN resolution metadata records final plenary component B at 55–1–1. This confirms that Soviet participation was not simply assigned to the UN/ROK side.

### 6. Resolution 711 was a composite outcome, not one undifferentiated vote
UN metadata separately records 711A at 43–5–10, 711B at 55–1–1, and 711C metadata at 54–3–1 for the communication component as catalogued by the UN record. The Index clarifies the committee/plenary mapping: A concerned membership/implementation of paragraph 60; B USSR participation; the India resolution was not voted in plenary at India's request; D concerned communication to PRC/DPRK and entered the adopted composite. This is why source labels must not be inferred from a bare `711C` without consulting the report/index apparatus.

### 7. A document-label trap was found and should become history debt
FRUS d752 calls the four committee resolutions A/B/C/D and says C was India and D communication. Current UN Digital Library resolution records expose final adopted components as A/B/C, while the resumed-session Index explicitly says the committee's India C was not voted and committee D was communication. Therefore `A/RES/711(VII)[C] = committee resolution C (India)` is false. The letter changed semantic referent across the committee-report/final-resolution stages. This should be modeled as a document-version lineage, not silently normalized.

### 8. The Soviet 11-power proposal can be anchored more precisely
The resumed-session Index identifies A/C.1/L.48 among political-conference membership documents, while contemporary scholarly indexing describes its original 18 August form as proposing the US, UK, France, USSR, PRC, India, Poland, Sweden, Burma, DPRK and ROK. Because the full UN original was not directly retrieved this pass, the exact wording remains `NARROWED`, not fully verified. Next target is the original A/C.1/L.48 and revision.
