# Pass 123 — CEB economic governance, exchange-rate agency, and postwar program

Date: 2026-09-24
Scope: Syngman Rhee backbone with non-presidential actor expansion. Floot not modified or built.

## Main findings

1. NIKH Rhee correspondence file `le_005_2310` identifies a 1953-07-08 Korean proposal to the Combined Economic Board on the hwan conversion-rate problem. Sender is Baek Du-jin in the explicit capacity `ROK Representative, Combined Economic Board`; recipient is Jack Bennett, `UNC Representative, Combined Economic Board`. This materially narrows Pass 122 lead 7: Baek was the named Korean CEB representative before the 1953-07-31 aid MOU.
2. The July 8 item is an attached document indexed to Baek, Bennett, Hanlon, Byun Young-tae, Tasca, the Cabinet, Bank of Korea, UNC and U.S. forces. Its subject indexing explicitly includes conversion rate of hwan and anti-inflationary program. It does not by itself establish Rhee authorship or prior instruction.
3. NIKH overseas-source document `fs_023_0120` describes the operational CEB as Prime Minister Baek Du-jin representing the ROK and C. Tyler Wood representing CINCUNC, with final approval authority over policy and projects, including goods and services in the aid program. Staff work was performed through U.S./UN members with Korean counterparts, while the Economic Coordinator planning director reviewed procurement requests funded by CRIK, FOA, or UNKRA.
4. The same source states FY1954 program figures of $80.6m CRIK and $30m U.S. share of a $45m UNKRA program, within a cited $325m total FOA/CRIK/UNKRA allocation. These are program-architecture facts, not evidence that Baek personally allocated each component.
5. The same source records a contemplated $450,000 three-year University of Minnesota contract with the Korean Government covering Seoul National University administration, medicine, engineering and agriculture, and a $2m UNKRA/California Polytechnic vocational-education contract. This expands ordinary-government coverage into education and human-capital reconstruction.
6. The December 14, 1953 reconstruction/financial-stabilization agreement is described as committing the ROK members of CEB to use best efforts to maximize Korean funds available for the common reconstruction/recovery task and to exchange information on essential-import activity. This is stronger evidence for an institutional Korean-side obligation than the earlier OCB retrospective summary alone.
7. The source describes concrete Korean stabilization measures: reduced budget expenditure, increased tax collection, and a reported 45% increase in 1953 total revenues. It also records unresolved cooperation disputes over exchange rate, coordination of foreign-exchange use, and Korean refusal to approve purchases in Japan with ROK/FOA-financed funds. These are contested implementation fields, not a single-person attribution.
8. Commercial procurement is described as Korean importers bidding for dollars, negotiating with suppliers in approved source areas, and using letters of credit through the Bank of Korea. This supplies a reusable institutional chain for later actor/event extraction.
9. Tasca mission FRUS d632 explicitly characterized the pre-reform CEB as formally broad but in practice focused on UNC-ROK economic relationship issues such as CRIK pricing, repayment for UN hwan drawings, and the military conversion rate. The mission recommended substantially greater attention to internal financial/economic policy and a new or amended aid agreement. This creates a before/after policy-design comparison with the July/December 1953 Korean-side proposals and agreements.
10. A UN report search surface independently describes CEB as the main aid-coordination forum with planning, finance, and relief-aid-goods-distribution subcommittees, constituted by the Economic Coordinator and Baek Du-jin for the ROK. Full PDF retrieval was blocked in this run, so the exact report text remains a follow-up target rather than counted as directly opened.
11. NIKH NARA-derived 1953-11-18 record identifies Air Force Chief Choi Yong-duk as an active participant in ROK-Japan security discussion, not merely a service-plan attendee. Choi argued that Korea could accept Japanese rearmament if Japan rearmed itself, but if the U.S. assisted Japan, Korea wanted its own forces built to at least comparable strength; he specifically raised concern over reports of a 1,700-aircraft Japanese force.
12. The Choi document also names Col. Kim Yong-hwan, commander of the 10th Fighter Wing, and Lt. Col. Park Chung-hoon, vice director of ROK Air Force operations. Park is a high-value future political/economic-administration crossover candidate and should be independently identified before promotion.
13. NIKH 1953-12-04 State Department telegram 1333 confirms Dulles responding to Rhee's November 10 letter on two linked problems: ROK-Japan relations and the economic aid program. This gives a concrete original-letter target for Rhee's own framing of aid and Japan policy.
14. NIKH `fs_023_0160`, although a 1955 implementation memorandum, preserves the implementation trail of the 1954 Agreed Minute: dollar sales through Bank of Korea and commodity-pricing disputes. It is useful as later-finding evidence and must not be silently projected backward onto 1953 decisions.

## Attribution firewalls / adversarial checks

1. Baek being ROK CEB representative does not make him author of every Korean CEB proposal.
2. July 8 proposal sender metadata does not prove Rhee prior instruction.
3. CEB final approval authority is institutional; do not assign every CEB approval personally to Baek or Wood without minutes/signatures.
4. Program-level CRIK/FOA/UNKRA allocations are not personal budget decisions by Baek.
5. The Minnesota/SNU contract under consideration is not evidence it was executed on the same terms.
6. Korean Government stabilization measures are not automatically presidential personal actions.
7. Reported 45% revenue increase requires underlying Korean fiscal series before treating it as independently verified outcome.
8. Japanese-source procurement refusal is an institutional policy dispute until the Korean decision chain is recovered.
9. Tasca mission recommendations are U.S. mission proposals, not adopted ROK policy by themselves.
10. UN report search extraction is not counted as a directly opened document because PDF retrieval returned 403.
11. Choi's 1953-11-18 comments establish his position in that meeting, not an Air Force-wide or Cabinet policy.
12. Park Chung-hoon's presence does not establish that he endorsed Choi's position.
13. Dulles's December 4 characterization of Rhee's November 10 letter is not a substitute for the original Rhee letter.
14. 1955 implementation evidence is later-finding evidence and cannot prove the exact 1953 implementation state.
