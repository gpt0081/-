# Pass 127 — Minnesota contract-chain / Korean actor expansion

Date: 2026-09-24
Floot: not modified or built.

## Why this branch was selected
Pass 126 left the SNU-Minnesota project as a verified project with an open Korean approval chain. Pass 127 followed that lead because it expands the corpus beyond presidential/diplomatic elites into education administration while also testing whether aid-project attribution can be separated into proposal, federal contract, Korean institutional agreement, execution, extension and CEB approval.

## Material findings

### 1. Three dates must not be collapsed into one agreement
University of Minnesota's institutional semi-annual report states that U.S. operations began under an interim contract on 1954-08-01 and that the University entered a three-year, $1.8 million contract with the U.S. Foreign Operations Administration on 1954-09-28. Operations in Korea began 1954-10-19 with arrival of the Chief Adviser.

Separately, Seoul National University's official historical photo publication captions a 1954-09-05 technical-assistance agreement ceremony and identifies SNU President Choi Kyu-nam at the ceremony. These records therefore point to at least two documentary layers, and probably three: Aug.1 interim U.S. contract, Sept.5 Korean university-level agreement/ceremony, and Sept.28 FOA-University of Minnesota federal contract. Until the originals are recovered, the project must not merge their signatories or legal effects.

### 2. Choi Kyu-nam becomes a substantive Korean actor node
SNU's official president profile identifies Choi Kyu-nam as 5th SNU president from 1951-09-03 to 1956-06-08 and later Minister of Education. SNU's own historical publication places him at the Sept.5, 1954 Minnesota technical-assistance agreement ceremony. The University of Minnesota semi-annual report further states that his December 1954 visit to Minnesota began a sustained institutional relationship. This is enough for a VERIFIED institutional actor node, but not enough to call him signatory to the Sept.28 FOA contract or sole Korean decision-maker.

### 3. Yun Il-sun enters the continuation/extension chain
The University of Minnesota project report states that Yun Il Sun had been SNU vice president, served as acting president during Choi's absences, and was appointed president in late June 1956. The same report records a Korean request for a two-year extension and later project-review conferences involving Yun. This makes him an independent actor in the continuation phase, while the identity of the formal Korean extension requester remains open.

### 4. The CEB authority problem is now project-specific
NIKH overseas-source material describes the Combined Economic Board, composed of the ROK and CINCUNC representatives, as the final approval institution for policies and projects in the overall aid program. This supports a legal/institutional reason to search CEB records for the Minnesota project. It does not establish that a particular SNU contract received a specific CEB vote. A project sheet, minutes or approval memorandum is still required.

### 5. 1957 governance reveals a Korean Advisory Committee
The Minnesota semi-annual report records a Korean Advisory Committee meeting on 1957-01-30 to study proposed ICA/Washington Amendment No.10, raise objections to three new provisions, and make recommendations to the President. Membership is not identified in the excerpt. This creates a concrete new institutional/personnel lead rather than an amorphous search for 'Korean involvement'.

### 6. Warne and Schneider provide execution-side foreign nodes
The same report identifies William E. Warne as the recently appointed UNC Economic Coordinator replacing C. Tyler Wood and Arthur E. Schneider as University of Minnesota Chief Adviser in Korea. They are registered as foreign execution/governance nodes because their records may expose the Korean counterpart chain. Neither is treated as a Korean politician.

## Adversarial checks
1. `Sept.5 SNU ceremony = Sept.28 FOA contract`: rejected pending originals; dates and institutional descriptions differ.
2. `Choi Kyu-nam signed the Sept.28 federal contract`: not established.
3. `FOA contract beneficiary SNU implies Korean government was a contracting party`: not established by recovered institutional report.
4. `CEB general final-approval authority proves a Minnesota-project CEB approval`: rejected; project-specific record still needed.
5. `Yun Il-sun formally requested the two-year extension`: not established; report says Korean authorities.
6. `Choi's later education-minister role can be back-projected to 1954`: rejected; he was SNU president during launch.
7. `Lee Seon-geun approved Minnesota Project because he was education minister`: not established; tenure overlap is only a lead.
8. `Korean Advisory Committee objections were accepted`: not established; final Amendment No.10 disposition remains open.
9. `William E. Warne's project consultation means he personally approved the original 1954 contract`: chronology contradicts this; he is documented in 1956 as Wood's successor.
10. `Minnesota Project was only faculty exchange`: contradicted by archival overview and reports describing advisory services, reconstruction and equipment as well as fellowships.

## Threshold honesty
This pass produced a substantive correction to the Minnesota-project event model and several new actor/institution leads, but it did not reach the requested 40 unique directly opened documents or 20 official-primary documents, and it did not deepen ten non-presidential Korean politicians. The strongest newly structured Korean actors are Choi Kyu-nam and Yun Il-sun; Lee Seon-geun is retained only as a primary-record target because office overlap is not action evidence.
