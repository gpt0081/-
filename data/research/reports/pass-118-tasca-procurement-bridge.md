# Pass 118 — Tasca procurement bridge during Hwang Jong-ryul tenure

Access date: 2026-09-24

## Why this pass matters
Pass 117 separated Hwang Jong-ryul's statutory authority as head of the Office of Foreign Procurement from his separate authority as head of the Provisional Office of Foreign Supply. This pass tests whether contemporaneous U.S./ROK records expose the actual procurement-policy interface during his tenure (1952-12-26 through 1953-05-28), without inferring that Hwang personally made decisions merely because he held the office.

## New archival anchors
1. National Archives of Korea international-aid collection lists a NARA-origin cable, **CAMG -> CINCUNC Japan: “Korean participation in procurement (Nr 20 to Tasca)”**, archive group CTA0001009, 1953. The item is public and belongs to `Cablegrams TASCA Mission (Outgoing & Incoming)`. This is the strongest new lead because its subject directly overlaps the legal portfolio of Hwang's Office of Foreign Procurement while Hwang was the incumbent.
2. The same CTA0001009 file contains **Nr 19 to Tasca** on the Korean Monetary Board and **Nr 21 to Tasca** on coordination of economic aid. These show that procurement participation was one component of a broader contemporaneous Tasca policy bundle covering finance and aid coordination.
3. CTA0001087 contains **Tasca 5, proposals for Korea** and **Tasca 9, analysis of the Korean economy and aid**. These are separate cable items and should be read against Nr 20 rather than treated as interchangeable summaries.
4. CTA0001480 contains a 1953 letter from **Kang Il-mae (姜一邁)** to Henry J. Tasca concerning import of aid cotton. The archive's original file title is `Commodities- Construction Material, Cotton (1953-1955) 2 of 2`. Kang is independently identified in the Korean History DB as born 1906-01-23 and later chairman of the textile association. This creates a private-industry/aid-procurement actor node, not yet a politician node.
5. National Archives of Korea photograph CET0024489 fixes a direct Rhee-Tasca meeting at Gyeongmudae on **1953-04-28**, inside Hwang's dual-office tenure.
6. FRUS Tasca Mission report states that Eisenhower appointed Henry J. Tasca on 1953-04-09; the mission arrived in Korea on 1953-04-17 and was tasked to recommend amounts/types of U.S. assistance, efficient utilization, and coordination of economic programs.
7. FRUS/ROK History DB fixes the Tasca report's NSC transmittal on **1953-06-23** (S/S-NSC files, lot 63 D 351, NSC 156 series). This is after Hwang left both offices on 1953-05-28, so report recommendations must not be back-projected as Hwang decisions.
8. FRUS later notes that the Tasca report, with minor revisions, became NSC 156/1 on 1953-07-17. This is also post-Hwang tenure.

## Claim/evidence consequences
### NARROWED: procurement-policy interface existed during Hwang tenure
The existence of `Nr 20 to Tasca: Korean participation in procurement` demonstrates that Korean participation in procurement was an explicit policy question being handled by the Tasca mission during Hwang's statutory tenure as Office of Foreign Procurement head. It does **not** yet prove Hwang authored, received, approved, opposed, or implemented the proposal.

### OPEN: Hwang's personal role in Nr 20
The web-accessible archive metadata does not expose the cable text. The archive viewer redirects to a PDF endpoint not retrievable in this run. Until the image/PDF is recovered, do not create a `proposed_by`, `decided_by`, or `opposed_by` edge for Hwang.

### OPEN: Rhee -> Hwang procurement instruction
Rhee met Tasca on 1953-04-28, but no source reviewed in this pass establishes that Rhee gave Hwang a procurement instruction arising from that meeting.

### OPEN: Korean-side Tasca interlocutors
The mission report and archive metadata establish the policy process, but the Korean participants in the Nr 19/20/21 discussions are not yet identified. Candidate targets include Hwang Jong-ryul, Prime Minister/Finance Minister Baek Du-jin, relevant Bank of Korea/Monetary Board members, and sector representatives. They remain candidates until the cable text identifies them.
