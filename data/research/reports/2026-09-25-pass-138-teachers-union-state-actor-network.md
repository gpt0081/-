# Pass 138 — 1960 teachers-union dispute becomes a multi-actor state-policy graph

Pass 137 established Lee Byeong-do as a cabinet actor and left the June 1960 teachers-union conflict as an event candidate. This pass followed that lead and found that the conflict cannot be represented responsibly as a one-minister story.

The Korea Democracy Foundation chronology records that on 1960-06-23 Education Minister Lee Byeong-do publicly demanded dissolution of teachers unions and warned of coercive action against continued organization or membership. Teacher-union representatives answered with a written rebuttal, argued legality under constitutional/labor-law grounds, recommended Lee's resignation, and threatened administrative litigation. A separate Foundation chronology records that the National Council of Trade Unions' dispute-guidance committee formally accused Lee on 1960-06-29 of violating the Labor Union Act. The legal outcome is not yet established.

The Foundation's dictionary entry materially widens the actor graph. Education vice-minister Lee Hang-nyeong had said on 1960-05-09 that the ministry would neither block nor encourage union formation. Health and Social Affairs vice-minister Kim Hak-muk said on 1960-06-18 that unions would be recognized upon notification. On 1960-06-22 Health and Social Affairs asked the Justice Ministry for a legal interpretation. This means the event must preserve inter-ministerial disagreement and changing positions rather than retroactively attributing one stable government policy to every official.

A second Education Ministry line also emerged. On 1960-05-07 Lee Byeong-do responded to post-April-Revolution campus rejection movements with a ministerial statement urging students not to engage in politics, educators to sever party ties, and creation of an Education Countermeasures Committee to pursue policy and legal reform. On 1960-07-01 the Education Ministry suspended Kyungpook National University medical school after a professor-abduction/confinement incident; vice-minister Lee Hang-nyeong publicly explained the action. These are separate education-administration events and should connect through person/organization nodes rather than being folded into the teachers-union event.

## Graph consequences

- `evt-1960-teachers-union-dispute` now has at least distinct Education Ministry, Health/Social Affairs Ministry, Justice Ministry, teacher-union, and broader labor-union edges.
- Lee Hang-nyeong qualifies for a senior-state-actor candidate node because he is independently tied to vice-ministerial policy statements and an administrative response.
- Kim Hak-muk qualifies for a senior-state-actor candidate node because he is independently tied to the Health and Social Affairs vice-minister office and a policy position in the inter-ministerial dispute.
- `evt-1960-education-democratization-response` and `evt-1960-campus-dispute-response` are separate candidate events.
- Lee Byeong-do's exact written authority remains OPEN: a press statement is not automatically a circular/order.
- The June 29 complaint's prosecutorial disposition remains OPEN.

## Adversarial result

The strongest correction is attributional. The evidence currently supports a sequence of competing public positions and institutional actions. It does not yet support a claim that Lee personally authored every Education Ministry measure, that Kim personally authored the June 22 legal inquiry, that Justice endorsed either ministry, or that the acting head of state personally directed the policy. Those questions require the actual ministry correspondence, cabinet records, legal opinion, personnel instruments and prosecutor/court records.
