# Pass 125 — Hanlon / Philip Han provenance correction

Date: 2026-09-24

## Scope
Followed Pass 124 leads on Admiral Hanlon, Philip Han, the July 1953 CEB correspondence chain, and the November 1953 ROKAF meeting. Floot untouched.

## Findings

### 1. Hanlon identity materially resolved
FRUS footnote to document 268 identifies the CEB official as **Rear Admiral Byron H. Hanlon, USN**, appointed CINCUNC representative on the Combined Economic Board in August 1952. A UN documentary search result independently states that Rear Admiral B. Hall Hanlon was appointed on 30 August as the CINCUNC representative, vice Major General Thomas W. Herren, and as Herren's Deputy for Civil Affairs. The UN PDF itself returned 403 on direct open, so the appointment-day detail remains provenance-qualified until the document is recovered through another archive.

This corrects Pass 124's generic `Hanlon` node. It also creates a sharper question: if Hanlon held the formal CINCUNC CEB appointment from August 1952, why does the 1953-07-08 Rhee correspondence metadata call Jack Bennett the UNC representative? Possible explanations include acting/delegated representation, a temporary replacement, or differing functional titles. Do not silently treat Bennett as Hanlon's formal successor.

### 2. Philip Han identity resolved to Han Pyo-wook
The NIKH explanatory PDF for the Rhee correspondence explicitly maps `Philip Han` to **韓豹頊**, and separately notes Rhee correspondence with 韓豹頊 among Korean diplomats. FRUS documents identify Philip Han as Counselor of the Korean Embassy on 1953-10-02 and again on 1954-02-09. This is sufficient to merge the Pass 124 `Philip Han` candidate with diplomat **한표욱 (Han Pyo-wook, 韓豹頊)** at VERIFIED identity level.

A later biographical notice gives 1916–2003 and says he joined the Washington embassy as first secretary in 1949, but this is secondary evidence and should not replace official personnel records for exact career chronology.

### 3. Philip Han had an action role, not only attendance
On 1954-02-09 Han personally delivered two sealed envelopes to Walter S. Robertson, one addressed to President Eisenhower and one to Robertson, carrying a Rhee letter and copy. This establishes an independently documented diplomatic courier/intermediary action. On 1953-10-02 he was present with Foreign Minister Pyun during substantive Korea-unification/security discussions. These records support an independent politician/diplomat node rather than treating him as a meeting-list accessory.

### 4. November 18 attribution remains narrow
The State Department memorandum names Choi Yong Duk, Kim Yong Hwan, Park Choong Hoon, Philip Han, E. F. Drumright and W. G. Jones. The text attributes the Japanese-rearmament/comparative-force argument to Choi and the U.S. response to Drumright. Attendance still must not be converted into endorsement by Kim, Park, Han or Jones.

### 5. CEB institutional contradiction / lead
NIKH `le_005_2310` explicitly labels Baek Du-jin as ROK Representative, CEB and Jack Bennett as UNC Representative on 1953-07-08. FRUS independently says Byron H. Hanlon had been appointed CINCUNC representative in August 1952. This is now a concrete role-title conflict requiring an appointment/delegation record, not a vague identity lead. Status: CONFLICTED/NARROWED, not resolved.

### 6. Economic-governance context retained
The FY55 Korea-program source describes the CEB, then composed of Prime Minister Baek and C. Tyler Wood, as final approval authority for program policies/projects, and records procurement, Bank of Korea letters of credit, budget/tax stabilization, Japanese procurement friction, counterpart funds, public-safety assistance, and education contracts. This supports keeping the CEB investigation distributed across finance, procurement, education, public administration and reconstruction rather than collapsing it into an exchange-rate-only story.
