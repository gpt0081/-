# Pass 136 — GSPA first intake and governance chronology

## Core correction

This pass found a high-value near-contemporaneous source that materially changes the cohort model inherited from Pass 135. Hahn-Been Lee's 1969 first-decade overview states that the first class enrolled in April 1959 contained **100 MPA candidates**, approximately 40 day students and 60 evening students. SNU's official current history separately states that the first MPA degrees were delivered to **80 graduates in March 1961**. These are therefore two separate bounded populations. No inference is made that the roughly twenty-person difference equals dropouts; individual admission and degree registers remain required.

The same Lee overview gives a pre-founding technical-assistance chain: SNU-University of Minnesota-ICA contract in February 1957; eleven mainly Law College faculty sent to Minnesota in August 1957; a second group one year later; first Minnesota adviser arriving March 1958; and project termination June 1962. This converts the former single 'founding' node into a sequence of technical-assistance, training, adviser-arrival, legal-establishment, operational-opening/intake, first-graduation and institutional-separation sub-events.

## Legal versus operational chronology

SNU GSPA's official history says the school was established by Presidential Decree in January 1959. An official GSPA brochure is more precise, giving **1959-01-13** as the establishment date. Lee's near-contemporaneous account says the new school was established in **April 1959**, in the same passage that describes enrollment of the first class. These statements should not be silently harmonized into one date. The working interpretation is legal establishment in January and operational opening/intake in April, pending the decree, first-semester calendar and opening records.

SNU's university-wide official history also treats GSPA's **1961 independence** as part of the later professional-graduate-school expansion. That supports keeping 1959 creation and 1961 separation/independence as different institutional acts. The exact March 1961 legal instrument is still open.

## Governance spine

The official GSPA brochure supplies a dean chronology that can now be used as an actor/network spine: Shin Tae-hwan (申泰煥), first dean, 1959-04-10 to 1960-09-19; Lee Byeong-do (李丙燾), acting dean, 1960-09-20 to 1961-03-31; Lee Yong-hui (李用熙), 1961-04-01 to 1962-02-10; Kim Jong-han (金鍾漢), 1962-02-11 to 1966-10-31; Hahn-Been Lee (李漢彬), 1966-11-01 to 1970-09-09; Cho Seok-jun (趙錫俊), acting, 1970-09-10 to 1970-11-25; Kim Yun-tae (金潤泰), 1970-11-26 to 1972-11-25; Park Dong-seo (朴東緖), 1972-11-26 to 1976-11-25; and Yu Hun (兪煇), 1976-11-26 to 1982-12-05.

These offices establish institutional responsibility, not authorship of decrees, curricula or admissions decisions. Each actor requires separate career reconstruction before politician-registry promotion.

## Politician-node threshold finding

Hahn-Been Lee is the clearest immediate cross-over actor. The Academy of Korean Studies' Encyclopedia of Korean Culture identifies him as a career official beginning as the Education Minister's secretary in 1948, then Planning Office budget chief, Finance Ministry budget director and vice-minister, later diplomat, GSPA professor/dean, and eventually Deputy Prime Minister and Economic Planning Board minister. This is sufficient to mark him as a verified senior-state-actor candidate for stable politician/bureaucrat registry promotion. Exact appointment dates and his role under the Rhee administration remain to be reconstructed from government records before a dense annals file is warranted.

## Adversarial findings

The strongest negative finding is methodological: first-intake size and first-graduate size must never again be used interchangeably. Likewise, the January/April 1959 dates are not evidence that one source is simply wrong. They describe different possible institutional states. The eleven Minnesota trainees cannot yet be called founding GSPA faculty, and dean tenure cannot be converted into decree or curriculum authorship without records of decision.
