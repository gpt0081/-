# Pass 122 — CEB, aid-control memorandum, and service-command network

Date: 2026-09-24
Scope: Syngman Rhee backbone with non-presidential actor expansion. Floot not modified or built.

## Main findings

1. FRUS XV/2 document 738 identifies the Republic of Korea Memorandum of Understanding dated 1953-07-31 and records that it was transmitted in Seoul telegram 121 of 1953-08-03 (895.00/8-353). The Korean Government sought U.S. concurrence in policies designed to place Korean-aid control under the Combined Economic Board (CEB), with American and Korean representatives as joint chairmen, and to have the CEB direct and coordinate existing relief and reconstruction bodies.
2. In the 1953-08-06 military/economic discussion, Prime Minister Paek Tu Chin asked Secretary Stevens for final U.S. approval of that memorandum. This is evidence of Paek advocating approval; it is not evidence that Paek personally authored the 1953-07-31 memorandum.
3. The same meeting names Defense Minister Son Won Il, Army Chief of Staff Paek Sun Yup, Navy Chief of Staff Park Ok Kyu, and Air Force Chief of Staff Choi Yong Duk. The Defense Minister summarized programs proposed by the three ROK armed services. Therefore service-plan authorship is retained at service level until original Army/Navy/Air Force submissions are recovered.
4. The Army requested implementation of a 20-division program with U.S. logistical support; Navy and Air Force programs sought forces sufficient for a balanced independent defense. Prime Minister Paek requested U.S. approval after Son's summary. This yields separate action edges: service proposed_by -> Son summarized_by -> Paek approval_requested_by.
5. FRUS XIV/2 and XV/2 parallel editions are treated as separate publication/provenance surfaces for the same consultation, not as independent witnesses to the underlying event.
6. The 1953-12-14 Wood–Paik agreement further specified CEB operating terms pursuant to the 1952 US-ROK Economic Coordination Agreement and 1948 Economic Cooperation Agreement. Later OCB reporting describes it as facilitating large-scale augmentation of U.S. economic assistance.
7. A United Nations Treaty Series retrospective text confirms that a 'Combined Economic Board Agreement for a Program of Economic Reconstruction and Financial Stabilization,' with annexes, was signed at Seoul on 1953-12-14 and remained legally relevant until superseded in 1961. This independently supports the existence and title of the agreement, but not every OCB characterization of its policy effect.
8. Harvard's Edward W. Wagner archival finding aid lists a 1953-12-14 Joint Press Statement on signing a program of economic reconstruction and financial stabilization. This is a concrete original-document target for the Korean/public presentation of the agreement.
9. FRUS document 740 shows Foreign Minister Pyun Yung Tai proposed text asserting complete ROK sovereignty over domestic affairs including the claimed right to drive Chinese Communist forces from Korean soil. Dulles rejected the formulation; Rhee and advisers accepted the objection and the clause was not retained. This is a verified individual proposal by Pyun, not merely attendance.
10. FRUS document 914 records Ambassador You Chan Yang delivering Rhee's signed 1954-07-01 letter on 1954-07-07 and saying he had urged Rhee to visit Washington. Minister Pyo Wook Han attended. Drafting or transmission work by Pyo remains unproven.
11. FRUS documents 924, 925 and 927 repeatedly identify the 1954 Washington Korean delegation: Rhee, Yang, Limb, Son Won-il, Paek Tu-chin, Choe Sun-chu, Chong Il-kwon, Kim Chong-yol and Choe Tok-sin. Repetition confirms delegation membership but does not establish individual policy authorship.
12. Document 927 records Dulles referring to General Won Yong Duk's warning to Communist NNSC members to leave the ROK. No recovered document in this pass establishes a prior Rhee order to Won. Superior-order attribution remains OPEN.

## Attribution firewalls / adversarial checks

1. 1953-07-31 MOU was a Korean Government memorandum; do not assign personal authorship to Rhee or Paek without Korean drafting/approval records.
2. Paek's request for U.S. approval does not prove he drafted the MOU.
3. Son's summary of service programs does not make him author of the Army/Navy/Air Force plans.
4. Presence of service chiefs does not by itself prove each personally drafted the service plan.
5. CEB joint-chair structure does not mean every aid decision was jointly approved by both chairmen.
6. OCB retrospective description of the 1953-12-14 agreement is a later U.S. executive assessment, not the signed text itself.
7. UNTS confirmation of the agreement's title/existence is not a substitute for the 1953 annexes.
8. Pyun's proposed sovereignty clause is his recorded proposal; do not automatically attribute it to Rhee as prior instruction.
9. Rhee/advisers accepting Dulles's objection does not establish unanimous internal agreement before the meeting.
10. Yang's delivery of Rhee's letter does not prove Yang drafted it.
11. Pyo's attendance does not prove drafting or transmission work.
12. Won's NNSC warning does not establish a Rhee-to-Won order.
13. Repeated 1954 delegation membership does not establish substantive intervention by each delegate.
14. Parallel FRUS volume publication of the same minutes is not counted as independent underlying-event corroboration.
