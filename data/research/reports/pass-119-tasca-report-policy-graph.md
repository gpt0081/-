# Pass 119 — Tasca report policy/authority decomposition

Access date: 2026-09-24

## Scope
Follow-up to pass 118. This pass moves from archive-item discovery to the published FRUS text of the Tasca Mission report and asks what the report actually says about Korean actors, institutions, policy domains, and decision authority. It does not back-project June recommendations into Hwang Jong-ryul's tenure and does not attribute Tasca recommendations to President Rhee.

## Findings with attribution controls
### Mission mandate and Korean consultation network
Eisenhower appointed Henry J. Tasca on 1953-04-09. The mission arrived 1953-04-17, based itself at UNCACK/KCAC in Busan, and consulted UNC agencies, UNKRA, **each ROK ministry**, chairmen of National Assembly committees, and the Korean Chamber of Commerce. The report explicitly singles out Prime Minister **Baek Du-jin (Paik Too Chin)** for cooperation. This upgrades Baek from a merely contextual Tasca candidate to a directly named Korean interlocutor in the published primary record.

This does **not** identify every ministry representative, committee chair, or Chamber participant. Those remain a high-value actor-discovery surface.

### The report is not a Hwang decision record
The report was dated 1953-06-15 and transmitted to the NSC on 1953-06-23. Hwang Jong-ryul's dual tenure ended 1953-05-28. Therefore the report is evidence for the mission's findings and recommendations, and for institutional questions investigated during the mission, but not by itself evidence that Hwang proposed, accepted, rejected, or executed those recommendations.

### Korean plan as an input, not Tasca invention
The report states that its recommended program was based in large part on the **ROK five-year plan**, modified to concentrate on essential production fields. This creates a new lead: recover the exact Korean five-year-plan version furnished to the mission and identify its Korean drafters, approving ministry/cabinet chain, and differences from the Tasca three-year program.

### Policy domains explicitly covered
The report creates strong event/claim candidates across at least these domains:
- defense/economic burden and 20-division force support;
- fiscal balance, taxation and expenditure control;
- monetary policy, credit and Monetary Board authority;
- exchange-rate unification and foreign-exchange governance;
- state-enterprise disposal/long lease and private enterprise;
- foreign trade transfer toward private operation;
- aid pricing and counterpart funds;
- offshore procurement and Korean participation in procurement;
- agriculture, fertilizer, irrigation, land use and reclamation;
- mining, geological survey and mineral exports;
- transport and industrial rehabilitation;
- public health, housing and education restoration;
- technical/vocational education and foreign advisers;
- aid-program governance through CEB and a proposed special representative/economic coordinator.

### CEB authority is distinct from procurement-office authority
The report says the existing UC-ROK Economic Coordination Agreement formally allowed CEB to consider broad aspects of the Korean economy, while its actual prior work had focused on UNC-ROK economic relations such as CRIK pricing, won/hwan advances and the military conversion rate. Tasca recommended a much larger CEB role in fiscal and economic policy. This is evidence against treating the Office of Foreign Procurement as the single decisive Korean negotiating node for the Tasca package.

### Monetary Board institutional edge
Tasca recommended greater Monetary Board authority over monetary and credit policy, participation in CEB deliberations, stronger bank supervision, and transfer of foreign-exchange policy authority to the Monetary Board. Pass 118's CTA0001009 `Nr 19 to Tasca` Monetary Board cable therefore belongs to a major policy strand visible in the final report, not an isolated cable.

### Procurement strand narrowed
The final report explicitly recommends that rationalized prices and an effective exchange rate open Korea to U.S. offshore procurement; Korean firms should be permitted to bid on items then procured largely in Japan; and Korean participation should be increased in aid planning and procurement. This materially narrows the interpretation of CTA0001009 `Nr 20 to Tasca: Korean participation in procurement`: the final policy strand concerns both Korean industrial participation in offshore procurement and Korean participation in aid-program planning/procurement. The original cable is still required before equating its exact subject with either paragraph.

### Political grievance around procurement
The report records substantial ROK-government dissatisfaction with UNKRA procurement methods because they excluded the Korean government from effective operation, and argues for a leading Korean role in procurement. This is a contemporaneous observation by the Tasca mission, not proof that Rhee or Hwang personally originated the complaint.

### Agriculture and social reconstruction
The mission endorsed broad FAO recommendations: fertilizer/pesticide expansion, irrigation repair/extension, land-use change, reclamation, and integrated management of domestic grain procurement and imported grain. It also recommended restoration of public health, minimum housing and education levels, and technical/vocational schools. These are useful correctives to an archive that otherwise risks overconcentrating on constitutional crises and coercive events.

## Adversarial checks
1. The final report is a U.S. mission recommendation, not a ROK decision.
2. Report date postdates Hwang's tenure; no back-projection.
3. Explicit thanks to Baek proves cooperation, not agreement with each recommendation.
4. `all ROK ministries` proves consultation breadth, not attendance by each minister personally.
5. CEB's formal broad remit differed from its actual narrow activity; preserve formal/operational distinction.
6. Procurement participation has at least two meanings in the report: offshore procurement by Korean firms and Korean participation in aid planning/procurement.
7. ROK dissatisfaction with UNKRA procurement is a mission observation; originator(s) remain unidentified.
8. Tasca's claim that its program was based on a ROK five-year plan requires recovery of the Korean source plan before assigning authorship.
9. Recommendations for privatization/free-market pricing should not be recorded as implemented policy without downstream Korean records.
10. Agricultural recommendations partly derive from FAO; provenance must preserve FAO -> Tasca adoption rather than attributing them wholly to Tasca.
11. Later OEC/FOA arrangements may reflect Tasca recommendations but require separate implementation evidence.
12. The report's economic diagnoses and projections are contemporaneous assessments, not verified outcomes.
