# Pass 128 — Korean-side Minnesota implementation chain

Date: 2026-09-24

## Why this pass
Pass 127 separated the 1954 SNU/Minnesota/FOA contract layers but left the Korean implementation side thin. Pass 128 therefore followed named Korean deans, ministry offices, veterinary expansion, and OEC-linked training records instead of repeating generic contract searches.

## Findings

### 1. Early Korean implementation leadership is now named
SNU Archives preserves two Jan.4, 1955 photographs/catalog records identifying Arthur E. Schneider with Hwang Young-mo (engineering dean), Lee Je-gu (medical dean), Cho Baek-hyun (agriculture dean), and Yun Il-sun (vice president). The same catalog description dates Schneider's chief-adviser appointment to Sept.5, 1954 and his Korea tenure from Oct.19, 1954 through June 30, 1961. This closes the prior chief-adviser identity gap but does not turn a photograph into proof of a joint decision.

### 2. The Korean-side scope-expansion negotiation is more concrete
A 2012 KSP government study states that after the project began, the Korean side submitted a proposal to ICA to expand the project. It describes repeated meetings involving the Korean Minister of Education, a Rehabilitation Ministry official, and the SNU president, with OEC, UNKRA and University of Minnesota representatives on the U.S./international side. Lee Seon-geun was the incumbent education minister, but the study names the office rather than the individual; exact personal participation remains NARROWED pending minutes.

The same KSP study states that SNU and Minnesota had signed before FOA executed the prime contract on Sept.28, 1954. This is a material refinement: Korean institutional signing is no longer wholly unsupported, but the original signature pages are still required to identify signers, capacities and relationship to the Sept.5 instrument.

### 3. Veterinary expansion supplies a rare actor-to-action chain
SNU Archives' Oh Soon-sup authority record says veterinary medicine was not in the original contract; Oh argued for inclusion, persuaded President Choi Kyu-nam, submitted a 250-item equipment list to Schneider in Jan.1956, and later undertook U.S. training. This provides a useful proposed_by → persuaded/approved-at-university-level → requested-equipment → trained/implemented chain, although the original petition and approval papers remain necessary.

SNU Archives also holds an original Feb.6, 1957 Schneider-signed document addressed to Oh and described as transmitting an OEC approval/certificate connected to his Minnesota veterinary training. This is an original-document lead that can anchor the OEC → adviser → Korean dean documentary chain.

### 4. Contemporary outcomes should remain separate from decisions
The Oct.15, 1958 대학신문 account preserved by SNU Archives reports faculty training, medical restructuring, facilities, public-health/nursing developments and veterinary expansion. It is valuable as contemporaneous observation but should not be used to infer who personally proposed or authorized each outcome.
