# Pass 124 — CEB proposal text, ROKAF actor identity, and provenance audit

Date: 2026-09-24
Floot: untouched.

## Scope

This pass continued Pass 123 without treating its 40-item queue as a checklist. The main objective was to convert several index-level associations into document-level actor/action evidence and to adversarially test identity and attribution claims.

## Directly verified findings

### 1. The 1953-07-08 CEB proposal is not merely an index hit

NIKH `dh_029_1953_07_08_0020`, reproducing the Rhee correspondence collection, contains the Korean text of Baek Du-jin's letter to UNC CEB representative Bennett. The opening explicitly says Baek is revisiting correspondence exchanged during his absence between Admiral Hanlon and Acting Prime Minister Pyun Yung-tai: Hanlon's 1953-05-29 letter and Pyun's 1953-06-10 reply. This creates two predecessor-document targets and shows that the July 8 proposal belongs to an ongoing institutional exchange rather than an isolated Baek initiative.

The same source identifies Baek as the Korean representative to the Combined Economic Board and Bennett as the UNC-side representative. The NIKH metadata for `le_005_2310` also indexes the Cabinet, Bank of Korea, UNC and U.S. forces in Korea, and the topics conversion rate of the hwan and anti-inflation policy.

Attribution rule: this supports `sent_by=Baek Du-jin` and a predecessor correspondence chain. It does not by itself prove who drafted the proposal, who inside the Cabinet or Bank of Korea authored particular provisions, or that Rhee personally ordered them.

### 2. The predecessor correspondence becomes a concrete document pair

The July 8 text gives exact targets: Admiral Hanlon -> Acting Prime Minister Pyun Yung-tai, 1953-05-29, and Pyun -> Hanlon, 1953-06-10. These are now higher-value leads than generic searches for 'CEB exchange rate'. They can test continuity of policy language, institutional authorship and whether the Bank of Korea position entered through Pyun, Baek or staff channels.

### 3. 1953-11-18 ROKAF memorandum resolves several actor roles

NIKH `hihd_1953_11_18_0030`, sourced to U.S. Department of State / RG 84 Seoul Embassy classified records, explicitly lists:

- Lt. Gen. Choi Yong Duk, Chief of Staff, ROK Air Force.
- Col. Kim Yong Hwan, Commander, 10th Fighter Wing, ROK Air Force.
- Lt. Col. Park Choong Hoon, Vice Director, Operation, ROK Air Force.
- Philip Han, Counselor of the Korean Embassy.
- E. F. Drumright, FE.
- W. G. Jones, NA.

The memorandum records Choi, not the other Korean participants, making the substantive statement that Korea could not object to Japanese rearmament if Japan rearmed itself, but if the United States assisted Japan then Korea wanted its forces built to comparable strength. Choi specifically raised the reported Japanese goal of a 1,700-aircraft air force.

Attribution rule: Choi is `speaker/proponent` for this recorded position. Kim, Park and Han are participants/observers unless another passage gives them separate speech. Their attendance must not be inflated into endorsement.

### 4. Kim Yong-hwan identity substantially verified, but date conflict preserved

The Academy of Korean Studies Encyclopedia entry identifies Kim Yong-hwan as 金英煥, a founding ROKAF figure, commander of the 10th Fighter Wing, and a brigadier general. It says he disappeared on 1954-03-05 and was legally treated as deceased later. This independently matches the November 1953 U.S. memorandum's `Col. Kim Yong Hwan, Commander 10th Fighter Wing` closely enough to raise the candidate from a name-only node to VERIFIED identity linkage.

A provenance conflict remains over birth/death presentation across later commemorative sources, so exact life-date fields should retain source-specific notes rather than silently normalizing them.

### 5. Park Choong-hoon crossover is strongly suggested, not yet fully closed

The 1953 State Department memorandum gives `Lt. Col. Park Choong Hoon, Vice Director, Operation ROK Air Force`. National Archives institutional-history material independently records a Park Chung-hoon serving as Commerce Minister in 1963 and 1964-67, and Presidential Archives bibliography identifies Park Chung-hoon as a later presidential acting authority. Secondary biographical sources describe the later statesman as a ROKAF major general.

This makes the crossover highly plausible, but the repository should still demand an official personnel record or equivalent source tying the 1953 Lt. Col. directly to the later minister/acting president before marking the identity chain fully RESOLVED.

### 6. December 14 CEB agreement status strengthened

UN Treaty Series Vol. 405 records that the `Combined Economic Board Agreement for a Program of Economic Reconstruction and Financial Stabilization`, with annexes, was signed in Seoul on 1953-12-14 and later superseded by the 1961 agreement. FRUS/OCB records independently state that C. Tyler Wood, as CINCUNC Economic Coordinator, and Prime Minister Paik Too Chin made the Dec. 14 agreement further specifying basic CEB operating terms. This is a cross-provenance confirmation of date, title/function, parties and annex existence, but the signed 1953 text and annexes remain unrecovered in this pass.

### 7. CEB friction points remain institutional, not presidentially attributable by default

The 1954 OCB progress report lists investment pace, anti-inflation imports versus investment, ROK foreign exchange, aid procurement especially in Japan, and dollar-hwan conversion as likely U.S.-ROK friction points. This is useful for coverage and lead generation but does not identify the Korean official who made each disputed decision. Those edges remain OPEN.

## Adversarial attribution checks

1. Baek as sender != Baek as sole drafter.
2. Baek as CEB representative != presidential authorization for every clause.
3. Hanlon/Pyun predecessor letters != proof that either originated the July proposal.
4. Bank of Korea indexed in the file != recovered Bank memorandum.
5. Pyun indexed != proof of his role in July 8 drafting beyond the explicitly referenced June 10 reply.
6. Tasca indexed != demonstrated action in the text currently recovered.
7. Choi's statement != Rhee instruction.
8. Kim Yong-hwan attendance != endorsement of Choi's Japan policy statement.
9. Park Choong-hoon attendance != endorsement.
10. Philip Han attendance != authorship or authorization.
11. 1,700-aircraft figure is recorded as a rumor/concern, not an established Japanese force plan in this document.
12. Later Park Chung-hoon career match != closed identity chain without personnel evidence.
13. FRUS summary of Dec. 14 agreement != substitute for signed agreement and annexes.
14. OCB description of friction points != Korean decision-chain evidence.
