# Pass 126 — CEB authority / ROKAF chronology cross-check

Date: 2026-09-24
Floot: not modified or built.

## Scope
Continued Pass 125's highest-value unresolved provenance lines while widening the network into education/reconstruction and military-to-civilian career continuity. This pass deliberately does not claim recovery of the missing May 29 Hanlon letter, June 10 Pyun reply, Jack Bennett delegation instrument, or 1953 CEB annexes.

## Material findings

### 1. Hanlon/Bennett remains a real authority conflict, not an identity typo
FRUS XV/1 doc.268 explicitly states that Rear Admiral Byron H. Hanlon, USN, was appointed CINCUNC representative on the Combined Economic Board in August 1952. NIKH `le_005_2310` independently labels Jack Bennett `UNC Representative, Combined Economic Board` on 1953-07-08 and identifies him as recipient of Baek Du-jin's exchange-rate proposal. No appointment/delegation instrument for Bennett was recovered in this pass. Status therefore remains CONFLICTED/NARROWED. Do not call Bennett Hanlon's successor.

### 2. July 8 proposal provenance is stronger but the drafting chain remains open
NIKH directly confirms Baek Du-jin as ROK Representative, CEB; Bennett as UNC Representative, CEB; date 1953-07-08; and indexed involvement of Hanlon, Pyun Yung-tai, Tasca, Cabinet, Bank of Korea and U.S. forces. This is enough to preserve the May 29 -> June 10 -> July 8 chain as a targeted provenance problem, but not enough to attribute drafting or approval to any indexed actor without the underlying referenced correspondence/attachments.

### 3. 1953 CEB agreement has treaty-series confirmation, but original annex text remains missing
UN Treaty Series vol.405 records the exact title `Combined Economic Board Agreement for a Program of Economic Reconstruction and Financial Stabilization`, says it had Annexes, and states it was signed at Seoul on 1953-12-14 between the Economic Coordinator of the Unified Command and the Prime Minister of the ROK. FRUS XV/2 doc.882 independently states that C. Tyler Wood and Prime Minister Paik Too Chin concluded the Dec.14 agreement pursuant to the 1952 Economic Coordination Agreement and 1948 Economic Cooperation Agreement. The 1961 treaty text confirms supersession, not the contents of the missing 1953 annexes.

### 4. Kim Yong-hwan chronology conflict sharpened
The Nov.18 State Department memorandum identifies Col. Kim Yong Hwan as commander of the 10th Fighter Wing. The Academy of Korean Studies biography independently identifies 김영환 (金英煥) as the first commander of the 10th Fighter Wing in 1953 and records his disappearance on 1954-03-05, but its header uses 1920 as birth year and administrative death year 1957. A War Memorial report carried by Yonhap gives 1921-01-08 to 1954-03-05. The project must retain the lifespan as CONFLICTED pending a service/personnel record rather than silently selecting one secondary chronology.

### 5. Park Choong-hoon identity continuity is materially stronger, but still needs a primary personnel bridge
The Nov.18 memorandum explicitly records Lt. Col. Park Choong Hoon as `Vice Director, Operation ROK Air Force`. Later biographical material consistently describes 박충훈 (朴忠勳, 1919-2001), later commerce minister/deputy prime minister/acting president, as a former ROKAF officer who retired as major general in 1961. This makes the identity match highly probable, but the project still requires an official ROKAF personnel chronology connecting the 1953 lieutenant-colonel post to the later civilian statesman before marking the bridge fully VERIFIED.

### 6. Han Pyo-wook was already an active presidential-instruction diplomatic node by April 1953
FRUS XV/1 doc.452 lists `Mr. Pyo Wook Han, Counselor, Embassy of Korea` in the 1953-04-08 Dulles/Yang meeting. Ambassador Yang was acting on President Rhee's instructions concerning armistice/security conditions. Han's presence does not prove authorship or endorsement, but it extends his independently documented diplomatic activity earlier than the Oct./Nov 1953 records used in Pass 125.

### 7. ROKAF meeting attribution remains speaker-specific
NIKH `hihd_1953_11_18_0030` gives the participant roster and attributes the Japanese-rearmament/comparative-force statement to Choi Yong Duk and the U.S. balance-of-power response to E. F. Drumright. Kim Yong Hwan, Park Choong Hoon, Philip Han and W. G. Jones remain participants/observers unless another record attributes speech or action to them. The 1,700-aircraft item remains a rumor reported by Choi, not a verified Japanese program.

### 8. Reconstruction/education becomes a separate policy branch
University of Minnesota archival material confirms the U.S.-sponsored SNU cooperative project began in 1954, with agriculture, engineering and medicine at its core and named university-side advisory structures. A later scholarly reconstruction reports an interim contract on 1954-08-01 and final contract on 1954-09-28. This branch should be connected to the CEB/FY55 aid-governance graph without assuming CEB approval details until Korean/FOA contract-review records are recovered.
