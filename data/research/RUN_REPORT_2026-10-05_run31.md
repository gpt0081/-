## Material findings
1. **The executive direct-election/bicameral program clearly predates the May 1952 coercive phase.** National Archives material identifies Cabinet approval in November 1951, Gazette No.562 publication on 1951-11-30, and Rhee's 1952-04-08 Cabinet instruction that direct presidential election and bicameralism should be implemented on time. This corrects any compressed chronology that makes the constitutional proposal itself a product of the May arrests.
2. **That earlier policy chronology does not erase the coercive phase.** FRUS telegram 1170 records Rhee saying he approved martial law, suggested Pusan be included, and that Won Yong-dok would take orders directly from him. The missing Korean written martial-law/command order remains an explicit evidentiary gap.
3. **Cabinet authorship must remain disaggregated.** The June 2 dissolution ultimatum is attributed to Rhee in FRUS doc.159; June 3 reporting from Prime Minister Chang Taek-sang describes cabinet members resisting Rhee's dissolution plan. Treating 'the cabinet' as a single author would destroy a politically important disagreement.
4. **The July 4 vote is now cross-checked across Korean and foreign provenance.** NIKH's contemporary Korean press record confirms passage and pressure on the 65 responsibility-government advocates; FRUS doc.206, citing UN A/2187, supplies the police roundup/escort chronology and 163-0-3 result; National Archives official constitutional history independently gives the 166-present/163-for result.
5. **The enacted amendment was hybrid.** The official promulgated text and archival explanation show direct presidential election together with bicameralism and cabinet/parliamentary responsibility elements. It should not be modeled as a one-clause direct-election event.
6. **Rhee's post-promulgation constitutional-operating preference is directly documented.** The July 8 Cabinet minute records his instruction that the revised constitution should still be operated as a presidential-centered system. This is stored as an executive position, not a legal conclusion about the constitution.
