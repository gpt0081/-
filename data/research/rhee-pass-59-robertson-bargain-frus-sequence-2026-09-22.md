# Rhee pass 59 — Robertson bargain, treaty drafting, and armistice cooperation

Date: 2026-09-22
Scope: 1953-06-29 through 1953-07-10, Robertson mission bargaining → ROK aide-mémoires → mutual-defense treaty drafting → POW/UNC cooperation → armistice acceptance threshold
Floot: untouched

## Direct-open corpus
Directly opened 40 distinct U.S. Department of State FRUS documents, Korea XV pt.2, d646–d685 inclusive. Accessed 2026-09-22. URLs follow `https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1952-54v15p2/d646` through `/d685`. This is a continuous official documentary sequence, not search snippets.

## Findings

1. **The bargain was iterative, not a single Rhee–Robertson agreement.** d646 shows Robertson rejecting the Korean June 28 aide-mémoire as inaccurate and returning it; Rhee, Paek Tu-chin and Pyun Yung-tai then undertook further revision. This makes each aide-mémoire a separate proposal/document state rather than a settled agreement.

2. **Washington contemplated pressure on Rhee through Korean elites, but that is not evidence such a move occurred.** d647 discusses creating an impression of possible UNC withdrawal and asks about the probability of ROK Army action preventing Rhee from sabotaging an armistice. Record this as U.S. contingency planning, not a Korean coup attempt or executed operation.

3. **Rhee's July 1 letter made post-political-conference resumption of fighting the core unresolved demand.** d651–d653 distinguish his requested U.S. pledge from what Robertson/Dulles were authorized to promise. The U.S. rejected an automatic commitment to resume war.

4. **Mutual-defense treaty language became a drafting negotiation of its own.** d656–d670 show successive U.S. formulations, questions over termination language and force stationing, and a July 6 State draft. d672 records Rhee proposing the U.S.–Japan security treaty as a model if faster Senate action was possible.

5. **Paek Tu-chin and Pyun Yung-tai were not interchangeable advisers.** d646 places both in the June 29 meeting. d662 again has Paek and Pyun present July 4. d676 reports Robertson's belief that Pyun drafted the July 7 Korean aide-mémoire and was trying to block agreement; this is an American contemporaneous attribution and must not be silently converted into established Korean authorship. d679 says Paek suggested a private Robertson–Rhee meeting without Pyun. These documents support distinct actor nodes and an intra-government advisory dynamic, but the exact Korean drafting chain still needs Korean-origin records.

6. **Rhee's written position moved materially by July 9.** d681 says the ROK would hold in abeyance pre-armistice demands for Chinese Communist withdrawal/unification, cooperate on remaining POW transport, and not obstruct the armistice under stated understandings. d683 is Robertson's contemporaneous itemization of the concessions. d684 says Washington regarded the letter as sufficient practical basis to proceed even though not wholly satisfactory.

7. **ROK forces under UNC and ROK sovereign freedom were deliberately left with residual ambiguity.** d677 records Rhee saying he did not intend to pull ROK forces out of UNC, while reserving sovereign freedom for a later disagreement. d685 records Clark's assumption that ROKA under UNC would remain so after armistice, while explicitly refusing a guarantee of full ROK government support or a commitment to use force against ROKA to compel compliance.

8. **POW implementation remained a live operational track during treaty diplomacy.** d675 and d680 cover moving Korean/Chinese non-repatriates toward the DMZ and the problem of Indian/NNRC presence. Treaty, command, POW custody, and political-conference questions therefore need linked but separate sub-events.

9. **NSC post-armistice policy is a separate U.S. policy layer.** d648, d673 and d674 develop NSC 157/154-series policy. These documents illuminate the external policy environment but should not be attributed to Rhee or Korean ministers absent Korean participation evidence.
