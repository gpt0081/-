## Material findings

### A. Decree 860 turns `establishment` into a committee-governance process
The original text of Presidential Decree 860 is materially richer than the prior index record. It establishes a **nine-person KDB Establishment Committee**. The Finance Minister commissions one member as chair. The committee, not a generic presidential actor, handles the articles of incorporation, demand for government capital payment, handover to the KDB governor, and other establishment business. Ordinary decisions require a majority of members present and a majority of those present; the articles of incorporation require at least two-thirds. The chair convenes meetings, coordinates agenda, and may create a secretariat.

This creates at least four actor layers that must not be collapsed: President/decree authority; Finance Minister appointment/approval authority; nine establishment commissioners; and the eventual KDB governor receiving the handover. The nine commissioners remain unnamed in the directly inspected sources and therefore become a high-priority actor-identification lead rather than guessed nodes.

### B. The capital-payment date is the legal establishment trigger
Law No.302 Article 55 says the establishment commissioners prepare the articles, obtain Finance Minister approval, and demand government payment; **KDB is established on the day that payment is made**. Decree 860 Article 9 then requires the committee to deposit the paid capital with a financial institution and issue a provisional receipt to the government. This means `1954-04-01 establishment` should not be modeled merely as a ceremonial launch date. The next evidentiary target is the government payment voucher/receipt or committee report that legally triggered establishment.

Decree 860 Article 10 requires the committee to dissolve within 30 days after establishment and hand over its documents. Article 11 requires a comprehensive establishment-progress report to the Finance Minister before dissolution. That report is now one of the highest-value missing primary documents because it should expose commissioner names, meeting sequence, capital payment and handover.

### C. Predecessor liquidation was institutionally coupled to KDB, not a simple rename
The liquidation decree states that Korea Industrial Bank dissolves on the KDB establishment date. The surviving text lineage shows the KDB governor serving as liquidator and a liquidation committee involving KDB plus Finance Ministry, Justice/legislative and banking actors. Although some displayed committee wording reflects later amendments and must not be silently back-projected to 1954, the decree's original legal architecture confirms that predecessor dissolution/liquidation was a separate process tied to KDB's establishment date.

Therefore `Korea Industrial Bank → KDB` must be decomposed into at least: statutory asset/liability succession under Law 302; predecessor dissolution on establishment date; liquidation; and KDB establishment/registration. Asset, liability and personnel succession should remain separate claims until the 1954 original decree text, transfer inventory and personnel records are recovered.

### D. Registration had its own judicial implementation layer
The 1954 promulgation index identifies Supreme Court Rule No.20, `한국산업은행등기처리규칙`, promulgated/effective 1954-02-24. This adds a judiciary/registration implementation domain between the January executive decrees and April establishment. It also supplies a new research line for the first actual KDB registration record and registration date.

### E. Gu Yong-seo's role can be stated more precisely, but appointment provenance remains open
BOK's official biography confirms 구용서 as first/second KDB governor in 1954. Combined with Decree 860, the governor is the recipient of the establishment committee's handover, while Decree 858 assigns the governor registration duties. This establishes a legal role for the office without proving the date, instrument or rationale of Gu's appointment. Those remain open and should be sourced from an appointment instrument, Official Gazette, personnel file or committee report.
