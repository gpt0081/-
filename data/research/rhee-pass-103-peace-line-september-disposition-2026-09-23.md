## Primary-source recovery
The NAHF diplomatic-document item `kj.d_0007_0010_0590`, **어업보호수역 선포에 관한 건**, was directly recovered as a text-bearing archival item rather than only a parent index.

Metadata and text establish:
- sender: Foreign Minister;
- recipient: President;
- date / drafting date: 1951-09-08;

- document type: official letter / draft;
- approval-date field: `Sept. 8. 1951`;
- the body states that the 98th temporary cabinet meeting on 1951-09-07 considered a proposal for the President to proclaim a fisheries-protection zone barring foreign fishing vessels and that the proposal passed **원안대로**;
- the Foreign Ministry then submitted the reasons/key points and boundary coordinates to the President.

This materially upgrades `evt-peaceline-1951-09-08-presidential-submission`: the submission itself is PRIMARY_TEXT_VERIFIED. It also proves that cabinet passage and presidential proclamation were distinct procedural stages.

## What the recovered text does NOT prove
The recovered transcription does not itself display a presidential rejection instruction. Therefore the Pass-102 wording `refused/rejected` remains too strong for a primary-source edge.

Two secondary/institutional research lines now converge on a narrower formulation:
1. The KCI-indexed article chronology labels the 1951-09-08 proposal `이승만 거부`.
2. A Korea Maritime Foundation / marine-education workshop PDF chronology labels the same 9/8 submission `대통령 결재 보류`, then records a new 1952-01-15 fourth cabinet meeting on the broader `인접해양에 대한 주권에 관한 선언`, followed by the 1/18 proclamation.

Because `거부` and `결재 보류` are not equivalent administrative acts, the claim is now **CONFLICTED/NARROWED** rather than simply OPEN: a presidential non-approval/interruption is supported by multiple later chronologies, but the exact disposition (reject / return / hold / no final approval) still requires the original approval marks, disposition sheet, or presidential/cabinet routing record.

3. Could the workshop chronology's `결재 보류` be a later interpretive paraphrase? Yes; retrieve its cited source chain.
4. Could cabinet approval have automatically created legal effect? The 9/8 document itself asks the President to declare it, so cabinet approval and proclamation were procedurally distinct.
5. Could the September proposal have been publicly proclaimed despite missing later references? No evidence recovered here; contemporary press/gazette search remains necessary.

9. Could the Foreign Ministry have revised the proposal without a presidential return order? Plausible; routing/version records needed.
