## Findings and adversarial checks
1. Clark reported screening estimates of roughly 70,000 repatriates while only about 39,000 had been selected by individual screening. These are command estimates, not verified completed totals (d175).
2. Clark requested permission to complete initial screening before neutral-country rescreening. This was a **request**, not proof that Washington granted authority or screening was completed (d175).
3. Clark distinguished neutral nations conducting screening from merely observing, and objected to Soviet participation. Proposed alternatives cannot be recorded as accepted armistice terms (d175).
4. Muccio reported Rhee's hostile reaction to the June 7 UNCURK letter, and described the Lee Bum-Suk group's growing influence over police. These are an ambassador's observations and inferences, not direct proof of a takeover or orders (d176).
5. Muccio reported Rhee telephoning his palace guard captain Kim concerning anti-foreign posters. The Korean words, instructions, and follow-through are not transcribed. No direct order can be inferred (d176).
6. Muccio identified Lee Bum-Suk, Paik Tu-Chin, and martial-law commander Won Yong-Duk as separate actors. Verify their Korean titles and action chains before stable actor linking (d176).
7. Hickerson's June 13 memo proposed further diplomatic pressure and, conditionally as a **last resort**, Clark assuming responsibility for Pusan security using Korean forces. This is a recommendation, **not evidence that the United States ordered or executed a military takeover** (d177).
8. Hickerson rejected compromise obtained under martial law threats as constitutionally insufficient. Compare against actual Korean Assembly votes, amendment texts and martial law termination (d177).
