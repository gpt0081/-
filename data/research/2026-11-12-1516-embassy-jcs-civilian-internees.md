## New findings and adversarial distinctions
1. **US embassy vs Washington policy split.** Lightner personally argues Rhee's replacement could be preferable and constitutional process paramount (d167); June 4 Washington State instruction d166 favored Rhee staying president under parliamentary checks. These are differing institutional/individual assessments, **not proof Washington ordered removal**. Mark CL-52-US-RHEE-REMOVAL NARROWED, not RESOLVED for all later dates.
2. **Embassy information bias expressly discussed.** Lightner acknowledged opposition figures contacted the embassy more frequently and claimed his staff also relayed government positions. Treat his social observations as attributed assessments, not representative public opinion statistics (d167).
3. **Administrative decision bottleneck.** Lightner described ministers and deputy ministers reluctant to decide without presidential referral and UNCACK decentralization difficulties (d167). This is one diplomatic observer's account, not proven comprehensive cabinet practice. Seek Korean cabinet minutes and UNCACK program files.
4. **Information policy not military intervention order.** JCS 910473 proposed interdepartmental public-opinion monitoring and possible psychological warfare specialists (d168), in response to Koje POW and armistice news. It does not establish these mechanisms were deployed.
5. **POW screening options not finalized.** JCS 910484 explicitly weighed neutral third-party interrogation before armistice, including two alternative implementation paths (d169). Do not conflate proposal with adopted policy.
6. **Civilian internees not POW category.** Clark advocated release of approximately 27,000 screened civilian internees, noting Rhee's repeated requests, and asked JCS views (d170). Clark's claim of decision authority is his own assertion. June 10 in-principle approval is an editorial footnote, not release-completion evidence.
