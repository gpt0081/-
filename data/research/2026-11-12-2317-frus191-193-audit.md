## New evidence and adversarial distinctions

1. FRUS 191 (June 25 State–JCS meeting, State–JCS Meetings lot 61 D 417) records contradictory recollections over whether the British were to be informed about hydroelectric bombing. The record itself is a State draft *not cleared with participants*. It does not settle the British government's account. It also records discussion of a prospective Clark/Muccio intervention telegram and concern that a reported assassination attempt could have been staged, which is **speculation**, not a finding.
2. FRUS 192 (June 25 JCS 912098, Matthews files lot 53 D 413) was approved as guidance, including Truman's added 'executive agent for UN' wording. It ordered contingency planning, sought views on reliability of Korean forces, and allowed emergency implementation under specified conditions. Approval of **contingency guidance** is not evidence of execution or of a specific Korean commander's complicity. Unlike earlier draft discussions, this is a transmitted directive.
3. FRUS 193 (June 28 Muccio 1474, 695A.0024/6-2852) summarizes Manhard's June 20–24 Chinese POW interviews on Koje. It differentiates interviewees' allegations, estimates, and direct observation; allegations of forced tattoos, intimidation, violence and manipulation of polling require camp logs, full interviews and ICRC/UNC verification. Do not universalize the interviewed group to all POWs.
