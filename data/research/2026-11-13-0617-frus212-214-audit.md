## Documents verified
- **DOC-FRUS-1952-212**, U. Alexis Johnson, memorandum, 1952-07-09, FE files lot 55 D 128, https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1952-54v15p1/d212 . Johnson questions the feasibility of Clark's neutral custody scheme: Swiss/Swedish reluctance, lack of guards/facilities, and opposition to prematurely promising not to send Chinese prisoners to Formosa. Handwritten annotation reports copy given to Gen. Collins for discussion with Clark; **delivery of copy is not adoption of the proposal**.
- **DOC-FRUS-1952-213**, State–JCS discussion memorandum (extract), 1952-07-09 11:00 Washington, State–JCS Meetings lot 61 D 417, https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1952-54v15p1/d213 . Bradley, Collins, Nitze, Bohlen, Johnson and others debate neutral supervision, screening location, guard logistics, prisoners' freedom to choose, and whether to advance Alternative B. The recorded discussion contains explicit doubts about the feasibility and Communist acceptance; no completed international agreement is evidenced. Bradley's speculative suggestion to list Chinese names to create an apparent larger number is a **discussion proposal**, not proof of falsified lists.
- **DOC-FRUS-1952-214**, Johnson draft memorandum, 1952-07-09, file 695A.0024/7-1752, https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1952-54v15p1/d214 . The document expressly assumes the Panikkar account is broadly accurate, develops a conditional interview/custody mechanism, and reproduces two differing versions of purported Chou 'Alternative B'. It is a **draft and derivative report**, not an authenticated Chinese government original or accepted armistice text.
