# Pass 154 — 2005 Article 58 labor-movement deletion resolved

## Material advance

The highest-priority open lead from Pass 153 is now resolved at the statutory-amendment level. The official National Law Information Center amendment record for **Private School Act Law No.7802 (promulgated 2005-12-29; effective 2006-07-01)** expressly states that the reform excluded labor movement from the grounds for dismissal of private-school teachers. The amendment text changed Article 58(1)(4) from the older form naming `정치운동 또는 노동운동` to a form beginning only with `정치운동`.

This corrects the temporal endpoint of the clause genealogy. The labor-movement dismissal language was not removed with teacher-union legalization in 1999. It remained a live statutory object into the late twentieth century, was still the subject of the Constitutional Court's 1999 97헌바61 decision concerning the 1981-version clause, and was deleted only by the 2005 amendment effective in 2006.

The official amendment rationale is unusually useful because it names the deletion itself among the reform's changes. But the evidence must not be stretched: chronology plus a general rationale does **not** yet prove that the 2005 deletion was causally coordinated with the 1999 Teacher Union Act. That causal/genealogical question remains OPEN pending National Assembly committee, substitute-bill, government-opinion and plenary records.

A post-amendment cross-check also shows the changed architecture: later Private School Act text omits 노동운동 from Article 58(1)(4), while Article 59 recognizes leave for teachers serving as full-time union officials under the Teacher Union Act. These are separate mechanisms and should not be collapsed.
