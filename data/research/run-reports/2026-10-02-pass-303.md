## Finding A · Won Yong Dok remains the strongest named Korean operational node, but the Korean order is still missing
The AKS anti-Communist POW entry independently describes the June 18–19 release as a Rhee-government-led mass release from multiple camps. A Dong-A historical feature explicitly names Provost Marshal Won Yong Dok as acting on Rhee's order. This is consistent with the stronger contemporaneous FRUS evidence opened in pass 302, where Clark reported Won issuing orders to ROK security units on June 19.

However, the present pass did **not** recover the Korean-language order, personnel order, camp circular, or unit diary. Therefore the graph must preserve three different evidentiary levels:
- Rhee decision/order: already supported by direct first-person/contemporaneous evidence from prior passes.

## Finding B · the Dec.24 parent event model is strengthened by Korean-source corroboration
AKS defines the `2·4파동` as the Dec.24 1958 episode in which opposition legislators were violently removed and the National Security Law and multiple other bills were passed. A separate AKS election-history entry states that on Dec.24 the Liberal Party changed the local-government system so that all local-government heads became appointive, reversing the earlier direct-election design for city/town/township heads. It also records the post-April-Revolution reversal to direct election in 1960.

This independently supports pass 302's FRUS-based conclusion that a single `evt-1958-1224-national-security-law` node is too narrow. The event should be modeled as a parent Assembly crisis with legislative subevents rather than as one security-law vote.

The local-autonomy subevent has a distinct institutional consequence: it altered how local executive offices were filled. That creates a separate research line for Interior Ministry/local-government actors, bill sponsors, committee members, and later reversal actors.

## Finding C · adversarial boundary on the Cho Bong-am/Jinbo Party context
The National Archives' Jinbo Party thematic page places the Dec.24 new National Security Law passage immediately before the Feb.27 1959 Supreme Court judgment in the Cho Bong-am case. It also distinguishes the Court's treatment of peaceful-unification advocacy from the espionage conviction. AKS separately records the broader Jinbo Party formation network.

This is useful context but does **not** prove that the Dec.24 amendment was enacted specifically to determine Cho's verdict, nor that Rhee personally ordered the Court outcome. Those stronger causal/direct-intervention claims remain separate and should not be inferred from chronology.
