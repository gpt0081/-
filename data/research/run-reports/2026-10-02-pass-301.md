## Findings
### A. 1953 anti-Communist POW release: presidential authorship is direct, downstream chain remains open
Rhee's 1953-06-18 letter to Gen. Mark Clark encloses the same-day public statement and explicitly says that he ordered the release on his own responsibility. The statement also says provincial governors and police officers were instructed to care for released prisoners. This is stronger than an external attribution: it is a contemporaneous first-person presidential record.

The same FRUS sequence independently records U.S. officials treating the release as Rhee's unilateral action. The 150th NSC discussion says the releases occurred simultaneously in four camps and therefore appeared planned in advance; Secretary Wilson additionally reported that ROK prisoner guards were under the Provost Marshal/internal-police line described as directly under Rhee. This narrows the execution-chain lead, but it does **not** identify the Korean written/telephone order, the Provost Marshal by name in the operative order, or each camp commander.

**Adversarial check:** searched for an alternative account in which UNC ordered, consented to, or covertly executed the release. The opened U.S. records instead stress surprise, repudiation, and Rhee's deliberate concealment from Clark. This supports presidential authorship of the release decision while leaving the precise Korean transmission chain open.

### B. 1953 Mutual Defense Treaty: Korean draft revisions can be separated from final agreement
FRUS doc.739 records Rhee circulating a new Korean draft. Dulles rejected a new fourth preambular clause and Rhee agreed to move the matter to a joint statement/letter. Dulles also required deletion of `effectively` from Article 3 because it diverged from the standard U.S. treaty formula. Doc.741 records Foreign Minister Pyun and Dulles initialing and exchanging the draft in the presence of Rhee and the ROK cabinet.

This gives a reusable sub-event model: **proposed_by Rhee/Korean side → objected/modified_by Dulles/U.S. side → accepted adjustment by Rhee → initialed_by Pyun and Dulles**. It prevents flattening the treaty into a single presidential act.

**Adversarial check:** the U.S. JCS record shows Washington's treaty offer had been conditioned on ROK cooperation with an armistice. Rhee's July 9 letter simultaneously promised earnest political cooperation while reserving Korean freedom of position. Therefore the treaty bargaining should not be recorded as a simple U.S. concession or a simple Korean victory; armistice compliance, reunification demands, and treaty language were linked bargaining tracks.

### C. 1958 National Security Law / 12·24 incident: knowledge and operational command must be split
FRUS doc.260 contains Ambassador Dowling's assessment that Rhee sincerely believed the amendment necessary, had been persuaded by Liberal Party leaders, and had **not been given the full truth** about the December 24 Assembly events. This is a contemporaneous diplomatic assessment, not proof of Rhee's subjective knowledge, but it is important contradicting evidence against any unqualified claim that Rhee personally knew every coercive operational detail in advance.

FRUS doc.272 later identifies Vice Speaker Han Hui-sok as one of the Liberal figures whose resignation was tied to settlement of the December 24 dispute. The FRUS index also places Han in the relevant National Security Law material. This strengthens Han's independent politician-node value beyond his 3·15 role.

**Adversarial check:** evidence that Rhee strongly supported the amendment coexists with Dowling's assessment of incomplete information about the Assembly coercion. Claim graph should therefore split `policy approval/support` from `advance knowledge/command of physical exclusion and police deployment`.

### D. Economic planning: the 3-year plan reached formal cabinet decision before regime collapse
National Archives record BA0085193, item 44-1, identifies the `경제개발 3개년 계획안(수정제)` as a 102-page document submitted by the Industrial Development Committee on 1960-03-31 and modified/approved by the 37th Cabinet meeting on 1960-04-15. The Archives overview separately states that a three-year development plan had been completed in 1959 and that 1950s planning institutions included the Planning Board and Ministry of Reconstruction.

This materially strengthens the non-crisis economic-administration coverage: the late Rhee government had a formal planning pipeline that reached cabinet modification/approval immediately before 4·19, even though implementation did not proceed under that government.

**Adversarial check:** National Archives explicitly distinguishes plan formation from implementation and says the 1950s plans were largely not implemented. Do not back-project later Five-Year Plan execution success onto the Rhee-era three-year plan.
