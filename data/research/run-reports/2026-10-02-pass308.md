# Pass 308 research ledger — 2026-10-02

## Scope
Continued directly from pass307's primary-source queue. This pass concentrated on the 1948-50 land-reform legislative chain and deliberately treated authorship as a multi-actor process rather than a presidential achievement label. Floot was not touched.

## Material findings
1. The land-reform chain now has at least four distinguishable drafting/decision layers: the Agriculture Ministry draft, Planning Office/government redraft, Assembly Industry-Agriculture committee alternative, and plenary amendments. NIKH constitutional-history material identifies Vice Minister Kang Jeong-taek and Land Bureau chief Kang Jin-guk as part of the Agriculture Ministry working team under Cho Bong-am, while the Planning Office, Legislation Office and Finance Ministry formed a separate government-review layer.
2. The Feb.15 Assembly record gives Lee Mun-won an identifiable legislative action: he argued that the land-reform bill had to be brought to the floor urgently. This is a concrete parliamentary act, not merely later association with the reform bloc.
3. On Mar.7 Kim Byeong-hoe and 69 others again requested introduction of an interim anti-disposal land measure. This creates a second parliamentary actor chain that should be kept separate from the main bill's drafting authorship.
4. The Mar.10 three-draft comparison is strong evidence against a one-author narrative. It places Agriculture Ministry, government, and committee texts side by side and shows materially different acquisition, compensation and distribution rules.
5. The government did not simply promulgate the Assembly text. A May 9 emergency cabinet meeting decided to return it, and a May 15 record lists eight requested revisions, including compensation instruments, eligibility, government power over distributed land and application to vested land.
6. The Jun.6 Assembly record shows a further constitutional/procedural dispute over whether the returned bill had lapsed during recess. The Agriculture Minister attended questioning. This should be modeled as a subevent distinct from substantive land-policy design.
7. The Jun.18 Ilmin Club statement asked for rapid promulgation even if the session had to be extended, showing that a pro-Rhee parliamentary grouping had its own recorded position rather than being collapsed into 'the government'.
8. Promulgation did not end the policy-making chain. The Industry-Agriculture committee submitted a broad amendment in Oct.1949; a Nov. record shows controversy over raising the farmer repayment ratio to 24/10; Jan.1950 plenary records show clause-by-clause amendment deliberation; and a Feb.1950 implementation decree draft contained 53 articles.
9. Later Supreme Court decisions are useful implementation evidence rather than mere retrospective commentary. The 1956 case treats the 1949-06-21 status of actual cultivation as central; the 1959 distribution case rejects an assumed general administrative cancellation power once distribution was fixed; the 1960 case distinguishes formal title from substantive self-cultivation; 1962 cases further define eligibility at implementation and a cultivator's pre-distribution cultivation right.
10. The current evidence therefore supports a graph model in which Cho Bong-am is a major ministerial sponsor, but not the sole drafter; Rhee is a consequential appointing/promulgating/executive actor, but not the sole legislative author; Assembly committee and plenary actors materially changed the bill; and implementation continued through later executive rules and adjudication.
