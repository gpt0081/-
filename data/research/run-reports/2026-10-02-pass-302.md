## Finding A · POW release downstream command chain is now materially narrower
Pass 301 established Rhee's personal decision/order from his own June 18 letter. Doc.612 now adds a named operational node: Clark reported that ROK Provost Marshal **Gen. Won Yong Dok (원용덕)** ordered ROK security units remaining near the camps to attack U.S. forces preventing further POW releases. Clark simultaneously recorded that Won acted directly under President Rhee and issued instructions separate from ROK Army Chief of Staff Paik's line.

This does not prove that every June 18 camp release order was transmitted personally by Won, but it is direct contemporaneous evidence that on June 19 he was issuing operational orders to the same ROK security units in furtherance of continued release attempts. Therefore the execution graph should no longer jump from `Rhee → anonymous guards`.

Provisional chain:
`Rhee decision/order (direct first-person evidence) → Won Yong Dok / Provost Marshal operational-security line (direct U.S. contemporaneous observation on June 19) → ROK security units at POW camps → camp-level release/escape execution`.

The final arrows remain NARROWED rather than RESOLVED until Korean orders, unit logs, or camp commander records identify the June 17–18 transmission.

Doc.612 also records a competing/parallel command fact: Gen. Paik, ROK Army Chief of Staff, understood Clark's intent to resist attacks, while Won was issuing separate instructions. This is important because it prevents falsely representing the entire ROK Army chain as a single undifferentiated presidential command channel.

### Adversarial checks
- Checked whether U.S./UNC forces themselves continued facilitating release after June 18. Doc.612 says U.S. troops replaced disaffected ROK security units and Clark ordered resistance to ROK attacks; doc.614 discusses preventing further escape. This contradicts UNC operational consent.
- Checked whether the ROK Chief of Staff and Provost Marshal can be collapsed into one command path. Doc.612 says the opposite: Paik and Won were on distinguishable lines.
- Checked whether `Won directly under Rhee` by itself proves Rhee personally issued Won's June 19 attack order. It does not. Preserve the distinction between organizational relationship and specific-order provenance.

## Finding B · 12·24 is not only a National Security Law event
FRUS doc.257 records that after police-reinforced Assembly Guards forcibly removed and confined opposition legislators, Liberal members passed the National Security Law **128–0**. The same sitting also passed a Local Autonomy Law amendment providing for appointment of mayors and town chiefs, even though opposition members plus five Liberals had rejected it in committee the previous day. It also notes a Councilors-law amendment delaying the upper house.

Korean History DB independently identifies the 1958 Local Autonomy Law amendment as shifting key local executive offices toward appointment, and notes the 1960 constitutional settlement restored direct election of city/town/township heads. Therefore `evt-1958-1224-national-security-law` is too narrow as a conceptual container unless it has sub-events or a broader parent event.

Recommended structure:
- parent: `evt-1958-1224-assembly-crisis`
- subevent A: opposition occupation / guard-police eviction and confinement
- subevent B: National Security Law amendment passage
- subevent C: Local Autonomy Law amendment passage
- subevent D: Councilors/upper-house implementation delay
- subevent E: U.S. diplomatic protest and Eisenhower message
- subevent F: subsequent Liberal–Democratic negotiation/settlement track

This matters for politician-node growth because the local-autonomy amendment connects the event to Interior Ministry/local-government actors, not merely party floor leaders and security-law sponsors.

## Finding C · Rhee knowledge claim can be narrowed further without exculpatory overreach
Doc.262 records Ambassador Yang telling Robertson that after a Dec.5 discussion he had personally made clear to Rhee that the press/speech provision should not suppress speech or press, and that Rhee agreed; Yang said the provision was then amended before passage. In the same conversation Robertson stated that U.S. interpretation was that Rhee may not have been fully informed about the Assembly developments.

Doc.264, however, records Rhee himself arguing that some departure from democratic processes was necessary in combating Communist infiltration and subversion. This prevents an opposite overcorrection in which incomplete operational knowledge is treated as opposition to coercive politics. The defensible claim split is now:
1. Rhee supported strengthening the security-law regime.
2. Rhee personally engaged with at least one contested press/speech provision before passage, according to Ambassador Yang's contemporaneous account.
3. U.S. diplomats believed he may not have known the full truth of the Dec.24 physical operation.
4. Rhee subsequently defended departures from democratic processes as sometimes necessary against Communism.
5. Exact advance knowledge/order of the police-reinforced eviction remains unproven in the opened material.
