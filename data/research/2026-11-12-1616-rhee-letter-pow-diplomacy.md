## Findings, attribution and counterchecks
1. Rhee's own June 5 letter minimized the constitutional crisis and characterized dissolution demands and direct-election support as nearly unanimous. **These are Rhee's assertions**, not independently verified public opinion or vote tallies. Rhee claimed all provincial legislatures south of the battlefront submitted dissolution resolutions; identify and inspect each provincial council record (d171).
2. Rhee wrote that he still sought settlement short of dissolving the Assembly. This is a contemporaneous stated intention, **not proof of actual restraint**; compare June–July arrests, assembly proceedings and legal decisions (d171).
3. FRUS editorial note says Acheson delivered the letter to Truman June 5 and advised no reply because Muccio already had instructions. Do not convert absence of reply into presidential approval of Rhee's characterization (d171 note 2).
4. Johnson's June 6 memorandum records British officials' account that Panikkar communicated an Eden draft to the PRC vice foreign minister and independently suggested neutral-territory POW rescreening, mentioning Kaesong. **Panikkar explicitly did not bind India** to this suggestion. Separate author, intermediary, receiver, and formal government adoption (d172).
5. Gross telegram 913 proposed discreet Soviet contacts; Kennan Moscow telegram 1966 of June 9 was referenced but not printed. Neither item proves that a Soviet approach actually occurred (d173).
6. FRUS editorial note d174 reports Truman's approval of Senator Russell's neutral observer commission suggestion, but the proposal failed. Approval of an initiative is not evidence of implemented observation; independently inspect Truman papers and invitations.
